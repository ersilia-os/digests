## 🤖 Models that could join the Hub

### 🎯 Activity prediction

- [Guirales-Medrano et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-11-17](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.15.766769) 🤖💻 — **Savant: A Fast, Structure-Free Binding Affinity Predictor For Protein-Ligand Drug Screening.** Open-source activity-prediction model taking a protein sequence plus ligand SMILES → predicted pKd; released with XGBoost weights in the repository under MIT. Plausible Hub addition because it needs no structure, no docking and no GPU — 100,000 compounds score in minutes on CPU, which is the deployment profile Hub partners actually have. · [code](https://github.com/sayalg/Savant) · 🎯

### 🧪 Property prediction

- [Tumescheit et al., *PLOS Comput Biol*, 2026-11-17](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1014925) 🤖💻 — **Ontology pre-training improves machine learning-based predictions for metabolites.** Open-source property-prediction model taking SMILES → MoleculeNet endpoints (solubility, lipophilicity, BBBP, Tox21, SIDER, ClinTox, BACE); released with code under MIT and checkpoints on Zenodo under CC-BY-4.0. Plausible Hub addition because inserting a ChEBI-ontology pre-training stage between general pretraining and fine-tuning lifts exactly the solubility and lipophilicity endpoints the Hub already serves, and the pretrained encoder is reusable as a featurizer. · [code](https://github.com/ChEB-AI/python-chebai) · 🧪
- [Shi et al., *chemRxiv*, 2026-11-18](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv.15012449) 🤖💻 — **UEFCCS: A Multimodal Deep Learning Framework with Fine-Tuned 3D Molecular Representations for Collision Cross Section Prediction.** Open-source property-prediction model taking SMILES plus an adduct type → collision cross section; released with weights on Hugging Face and inference code on GitHub under MIT. Plausible Hub addition because CCS is a clean single-number endpoint with no Hub coverage, and it doubles as a metabolomics-identification filter. · [code](https://github.com/ypsh1/UEFCCS) · 🧪
- [Suresh Jyothi Bai et al., *BioMedInformatics*, 2026-11-19](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-7426/6/6/112) 🌍🤖💻 — **Fingerprint–Descriptor Fusion Improves Scaffold-Stratified External Performance in Blood–Brain Barrier Permeability Prediction.** Open-source property-prediction model taking SMILES → binary blood–brain-barrier call with SHAP attribution, from a fused 2,058-dimensional Morgan-plus-descriptor vector; released with code under MIT on GitHub and archived on Zenodo under CC-BY-4.0 (weights: pending — the archive ships configurations, partitions and held-out predictions rather than fitted artefacts). Plausible Hub addition because it is validated on a scaffold-disjoint external set from B3DB rather than a random split, which is where most BBB models quietly fall over. · [code](https://github.com/Rithikkaa-17/-BBB_binary_classification) · 🧪

### 🧩 Featurization

- [Lee et al., *arXiv*, 2026-11-16](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21276) 🤖💻 — **Classical structural priors improve neural molecular representations in molecule language models (SAGA).** Open-source featurization method taking SMILES → molecular embeddings, aligning a molecule-language model's embedding geometry to fingerprint Tanimoto similarity during training only, so nothing extra is needed at inference; code on GitHub under MIT (weights: pending). Plausible Hub addition because it improves retrieval, captioning and scaffold-split property prediction without changing the inference interface of an existing encoder. · [code](https://github.com/February24-Lee/SAGA) · 🧩

### 🎨 Generation

- [Forooghi et al., *arXiv*, 2026-11-13](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17650) 🤖💻 — **Classifier-Guided Latent Diffusion for Small-Molecule Generation.** Open-source generative model taking a frozen ChemBERTa latent → RDKit-valid SMILES, steered at sampling time by a differentiable property classifier toward blood–brain-barrier permeability or BACE-1 inhibition; code on GitHub under MIT (weights: pending). Plausible Hub addition because property-steered generation needs no retraining per objective, and generation is the Hub's fastest-growing task family. · [code](https://github.com/FlockOfBird/GMGD) · 🎨

### 🔍 Similarity search

- [Somers et al., *arXiv*, 2026-11-18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25076) 🤖💻 — **ShEPhERD-score: Fast, adaptable three-dimensional alignment of molecular features.** Open-source similarity model taking 3D molecular conformers → shape, electrostatic and pharmacophore similarity scores with optimal alignment, at up to a million alignments per second on one GPU; released as the versioned, pip-installable shepherd-score v2.0 package under MIT. Plausible Hub addition because similarity search is one of the Hub's two thinnest task families and this is a maintained package rather than paper code. · [code](https://github.com/coleygroup/shepherd-score) · 🔍

## 🗃️ Datasets that could join the Hub

### Generative training corpora

- [Williams et al., *arXiv*, 2026-11-18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24996) 🗃️💻 — **Chemistry by Design (CbyD): A Machine-Readable Dataset of Expert-Curated Total Synthesis Routes.** 3,502 routes · 56,640 expert-curated reactions over 2,902 natural-product and drug targets · CC-BY-4.0 · GitHub. Plausible Hub input because its reactions are measurably more complex and less template-covered than USPTO-derived sets, which is exactly where patent-trained retrosynthesis models fail. · [code](https://github.com/chemistry-by-design/cbyd) · 🎨

### Other Hub-relevant datasets

- [Lee et al., *Sci Data*, 2026-11-16](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-08750-7) 🗃️ — **Dataset of Anticancer Drug Responses in Mouse Xenograft Models.** 1,602 xenograft cases curated from 143 publications · tumour volume and weight versus control, plus body-weight loss · CC-BY-4.0 · Mendeley Data. Below the usual size bar, but open in vivo efficacy–toxicity data at this granularity barely exists, and it is the kind of endpoint no Hub model currently covers. · 🎯

## AI/ML methods for drug discovery

- [Kopf et al., *Nature Computational Science*, 2026-11-19](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-026-01082-x) — **Generative molecular design needs oracle grounding.** Argues that generative-design benchmarks are only as good as the oracles scoring them, and that ungrounded oracles manufacture progress that does not survive contact with an assay. Directly relevant to how Hub generative entries should be evaluated before incorporation.
- [Gallagher et al., *Nature Computational Science*, 2026-11-18](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-026-01076-9) — **Minimizing data requirements for chemical and material property prediction via cluster-based training set selection.** Across 100 selection algorithms and 60 property tasks, cluster-based training-set selection consistently beats active learning when picking from a fixed candidate library — a direct challenge to the default active-learning loop used in low-data property modelling.
- [Fan et al., *J Chem Inf Model*, 2026-11-18](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.6c02793) — **Artificial Intelligence for Generating Drug-like Molecules: A Taxonomy of Methods and Evaluation Strategies.** Workflow-level review of generative design — datasets, representation, conditioning, oracles, prioritisation — rather than another architecture survey. Useful as the reference frame when triaging generator papers for the Hub.
- [Liu et al., *J Chem Inf Model*, 2026-11-17](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.6c03060) — **HiFiPL-Bench: A Fidelity-First and Bias-Aware Benchmark for Diagnosing Protein-Ligand Scoring Functions.** Rebuilds a protein–ligand benchmark with electron-density validation and property-matched decoys, and shows nine scoring functions do worse than CASF-2016 suggests. Part of the benchmark-auditing thread worth reading before trusting any docking-surrogate leaderboard.
- [Janet, *Chem Sci*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1039/d6sc06542b) — **Generative models in industrial drug discovery: perspectives from REINVENT.** A practitioner's account of what generative chemistry actually delivers inside a pharma pipeline, from the team behind one of the most widely reused open generators.
- [Alkhoury et al., *J Cheminform*, 2026-11-18](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13321-026-01328-w) 💻 — **Feature selection-guided graph neural networks for compound activity prediction.** Pre-selects a ranked, chemically interpretable descriptor subset and feeds it alongside the molecular graph, from the Bajorath group. (License: the repository carries none, so it cannot be redistributed as a Hub entry as it stands.) · [code](https://github.com/FouadAlkhoury/MolecularFeatureRanking)
- [Wen et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-11-19](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.18.767182) 💻 — **AIO Part I: Interpretable ADMET Prediction for Cyclic Peptides and Macrocycles.** Multimodal ADMET framework for beyond-Rule-of-5 space with solvent-aware pretraining and segment-level attribution, reporting R = 0.87 on PAMPA permeability. (License: CC-BY-NC, and the released weights are encrypted behind an author-issued, expiring access key — not incorporable despite the open repository.) · [code](https://github.com/wsicheng739/AIO-ADMET)
- [Huang et al., *ACS Omega*, 2026-11-16](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.6c09697) — **Mixture-of-Prompts for Parameter-Efficient ADMET Prediction.** Keeps a molecular foundation model frozen and learns a mixture of prompts per endpoint, evaluated on PharmaBench and external OpenADMET sets. No repository could be confirmed from the article page — worth a manual check, since parameter-efficient adaptation is how one backbone could serve many Hub endpoints.
- [Yang et al., *Bioinformatics*, 2026-11-16](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btag838) 💻 — **ReMoD-Mol: Reliability-Aware Multimodal Learning with Descriptor-Guided Knowledge Distillation for Molecular Property Prediction.** Fuses graphs, SMILES and 2D images with per-benchmark modality weighting and descriptor-teacher distillation. (License: none declared on the repo — not redistributable as a Hub entry until one is added.) · [code](https://github.com/27167199/ReMoD-Mol)
- [Guo et al., *ACS Chem Biol*, 2026-11-16](https://doi.org/10.1021/acschembio.6c00553) — **Chemical Biology in the Age of Artificial Intelligence: From Ligand Discovery to Virtual Cells.** An orientation piece covering featurization, model families, accuracy assessment and agentic systems — a reasonable single reference to hand a new team scientist.
- [Jones et al., *Mol Pharm*, 2026-11-12](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.molpharmaceut.6c01212) — **Novel Molecular Descriptors for ADME Prediction.** Introduces descriptors aimed at ADME endpoints including chromatographic logD, from the Simulations Plus group. ChromLogD is a Hub gap, though the descriptor set itself is tied to a commercial stack.
- [García-Cerqueda et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-11-11](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.10.765562) 💻 — **Pocket similarity-guided selection of protein targets for systematic protein–ligand dataset generation.** Uses PocketVec to map 51,535 druggable human pockets into 1,865 clusters and picks two diverse 200-pocket sets for Target 2035-scale open binding-data generation, from IRB Barcelona. The selections are public but the repository carries no licence yet. · [code](https://github.com/sbnb-irb/Target_2035)
- [Shu et al., *chemRxiv*, 2026-11-18](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv.15012428) — **AIMETRY: Geometry-sensitive 3D molecular representations from quantum mechanical learned features.** Turns AIMNet2 quantum-mechanical atomic features into geometry-aware molecular embeddings, pretrained over 44M conformers of 2.8M ChEMBL molecules, from the Isayev lab. (No repository or weights named in the preprint — worth a second look as a featurizer once a release lands.)
- [Paul et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-11-18](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.18.767483) — **Quantifying Homology and Length Bias in Antimicrobial Peptide Prediction.** A length-only classifier beats chance on standard AMP benchmarks, and imposing homology and length controls drops a 0.834 MCC to 0.661. (Input: peptide sequence.) The protocol transfers to any Hub benchmark where negatives were never matched to positives.

## Antibiotic and antimicrobial discovery

- [Luttens et al., *Nature Reviews Microbiology*, 2026-11-17](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41579-026-01393-w) — **Artificial intelligence for antibiotic discovery and design.** Review of AI-enabled small-molecule antibacterial discovery — phenotypic screening, mechanism-of-action elucidation, target-based discovery, de novo design and lead optimisation — from the Collins group. The closest thing to a map of the field the Ersilia AMR pipelines sit in.
- [Catacutan et al., *Clin Microbiol Infect*, 2026-11-16](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmi.2026.11.006) — **Machine learning for small-molecule antibiotics — discovery to pre-clinical optimization.** Companion review from the Stokes lab, organised along the preclinical pipeline and explicit that ML's contribution here remains unproven. Useful calibration for claims made about antibacterial activity models.
- [Hashimoto et al., *PNAS*, 2026-11-19](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2619331123) ⭐ — **Linear plasmids mediate antimicrobial resistance gene transfer between environmental bacteria and hospital-adapted enterococci.** Traces clinically relevant resistance genes back to environmental reservoirs via a previously under-appreciated class of mobile element.
- [Zhou et al., *ACS Infect Dis*, 2026-11-13](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsinfecdis.6c00764) — **Tyrosine Amide and Triazaspirocyclic Antimalarials Are Resistant to the Mutant Plasmodium falciparum Cyclic Amine Resistance Locus.** Two HTS-derived antimalarial chemotypes whose resistance maps to PfCARL and PfUDP but which retain activity against the KAF156-resistant mutant — new scaffolds for antimalarial activity models.
- [Lacasse et al., *ACS Infect Dis*, 2026-11-13](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsinfecdis.6c00628) — **Bis-Catechol Siderophores Modulate β-Lactam Acylation at the Active Site of Pseudomonas aeruginosa Penicillin-Binding Protein 3.** Shows siderophore conjugation works partly through PBP3 affinity, not only through uptake — a mechanistic correction relevant to Gram-negative permeability work.
- [Tammour et al., *Int J Parasitol Drugs Drug Resist*, 2026-12](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpddr.2026.100673) — **Repurposed small-molecule inhibitors of Plasmodium falciparum calmodulins disrupt calcium homeostasis.** Repurposing hits against an unexploited *P. falciparum* target class, with a phenotype that ties back to calcium signalling.
- [Magizhvannan et al., *Front Cell Infect Microbiol*, 2026-11-18](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcimb.2026.1934561) 🌍 — **LSTM-assisted machine learning framework for low-data prioritization of CepI-targeted candidates.** Anti-virulence prioritisation against the *Burkholderia cenocepacia* quorum-sensing synthase CepI, built deliberately for a low-data target — the regime most Hub-relevant NTD endpoints live in.
- [Liu et al., *Front Pharmacol*, 2026-11-12](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2026.1979931) — **Repurposing the cystic fibrosis drug ivacaftor as an efflux pump inhibitor to inhibit antimicrobial resistance in Pseudomonas aeruginosa.** Ivacaftor inhibits MexAB–OprM, extending an earlier AcrB result; efflux-pump inhibition remains a thin area in open activity models.
- [Pruhlo & Iurchenko, *Pharmazie*, 2026-11-13](https://doi.org/10.31083/Pharmazie55687) — **ATC Classification and Scaffold-Split pMIC Prediction for Antibacterial Prioritization.** Scaffold-split *S. aureus* pMIC regression over 33,505 ChEMBL compounds (R² 0.51) plus an ATC-label classifier, with the authors explicit that the classifier flags drug-like resemblance rather than measured activity. No code released, but the scaffold-split protocol and the honesty about what the labels mean are worth borrowing.
- [Eker & AlMatar, *Curr Pharm Des*, 2026-11-10](https://doi.org/10.2174/0113816128531981261103073324) — **Targeted Protein Degradation as a Novel Drug Discovery Platform for Antimicrobial Resistance: PROTACs, BacPROTACs.** Review of degradation-based antibacterials, the modality behind the ClpC/ClpP-directed work Ersilia supports on the SAMRC BacPROTAC track.

## AI agents and foundation models for science

- [Inizan et al., *Nature Computational Science*, 2026-11-20](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-026-01058-x) — **System of agentic AI for the discovery of metal–organic frameworks.** A multi-agent system driving a real materials-discovery campaign end to end, from the Yaghi group. The orchestration pattern is the transferable part for chemistry agents over the Hub.
- [Merzbacher et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-11-19](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.18.767545) — **Surrogate Verifiers Shape Decisions in Closed-Loop Experimental Design.** Asks when a learned surrogate can stand in for a wet assay inside a self-driving lab, and shows the answer depends on calibrated uncertainty and extrapolation behaviour rather than headline accuracy.
- [Li et al., *Research Square*, 2026-11-17](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-9959783/v1) — **Agentic reinforcement learning empowers next-generation chemical language models for molecular design and synthesis.** ChemCRAFT trains a small, locally deployable model to call chemistry tools in a sandbox rather than memorise chemistry — the architecture an MCP-exposed model registry would want on the other end.
- [Li et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-11-21](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.17.767465) — **MicroBioAgent: an AI agent for automated microbiome analysis.** Turns high-level biological questions into executable bioinformatics pipelines; a concrete template for wrapping a heterogeneous toolchain behind one agent interface.
- [Prior Labs, *technical report*, 2026-11-19](https://priorlabs.ai/technical-reports/tabpfn-4-preview) — **TabPFN-4: The next step for predictive intelligence.** A sub-quadratic architecture that takes in-context tabular prediction to 10 million training rows and leads most public tabular benchmarks, including the biology set. Weights are waitlist-only with no licence stated, and earlier releases were non-commercial — relevant to on-demand prediction over stored bioactivity, but not something that can be redistributed today.
- [Lin et al., *Nat Prod Rep*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1039/d5np00086f) — **The complexity cliff in natural product discovery: why full automation remains elusive and the case for collaborative intelligence.** Argues automation degrades discontinuously, not gradually, once chemical or biological complexity passes what a standardised pipeline assumes — a useful corrective to self-driving-lab enthusiasm.

## Global health and open science

- [Smith et al., *Nature Computational Science*, 2026-11-19](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-026-01084-9) 🌍 — **An alternative path for artificial intelligence in Africa.** A 21-author comment, most of them at African institutions, rejecting the choice between costly national data centres and dependence on foreign cloud providers, and proposing pooled "distributed sovereignty" infrastructure designed for African conditions instead.
- [Alegana et al., *BMJ Global Health*, 2026-11-11](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjgh-2026-025607) 🌍 — **Cluster-level convergence of malaria infection, childhood zero-dose prevalence and health-campaign frequency in sub-Saharan Africa.** WHO AFRO-led Bayesian analysis of 8,396 survey clusters across 18 countries: malaria positivity and unvaccinated children co-occur in 12 of them, and three quarters of the worst-affected Admin-1 units already sit in the highest campaign-frequency tertile — the gap is coordination, not coverage. 338 campaign pairs that could have been delivered together were run separately.
- [Zhu et al., *BMJ*, 2026-11-20](https://www.bmj.com/content/395/bmj-2026-101501) — **Capturing the value of antimicrobial resistance innovation.** A BMJ editorial from the Fleming Initiative, with a co-author at Brazil's health ministry, on how the value of antimicrobial innovation should be recognised and rewarded.
- [Adefolalu, *Infect Drug Resist*, 2026-11-15](https://doi.org/10.2147/IDR.S652377) — **One Health Education for Zoonotic Disease and Antimicrobial Resistance Control in Southern Africa: A Constraints-First Integrative Review.** Argues the competency that matters is not generic AMR awareness but the ability to route a resistance signal between human, animal and environmental compartments, and notes South Africa still has no routine livestock AMR surveillance.
- [Absar et al., *Sci Data*, 2026-11-14](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-08643-9) 🌍 — **BDMedData: A Multi-Therapeutic Drug Dataset for Pharmacoinformatics and Public Health in Bangladesh.** Over 50,000 medication entries scraped from Bangladesh's national drug registry across allopathic, ayurvedic, herbal, homeopathic and unani systems, released on Kaggle under CC0 by a team at Independent University, Bangladesh. No structures or endpoints, so not Hub-trainable, but a rare open national formulary for access and pricing work.

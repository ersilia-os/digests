## 🤖 Models that could join the Hub

### 🎯 Activity prediction

- [Huang et al., *Communications Chemistry*, 2026-09-08](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42004-026-02176-3) 🤖 — **Multi-source language models fused with a dynamic routing capsule network for antimicrobial compound identification.** Open antimicrobial activity model taking SMILES → inhibition against *Escherichia coli* and *Acinetobacter baumannii*; five chemistry language models are fused through a dynamic-routing capsule head and served from a public web portal (CapMolPred). Plausible Hub addition because whole-cell Gram-negative activity predictors are the scarcest class in the Hub and map directly onto the E-AMR-CC track — the article sits under CC-BY-NC-ND and no code licence is declared, so licence clarification is the gating step before packaging. · 🎯
- [Zeng et al., *Molecular Diversity*, 2026-09-03](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11030-026-11709-w) 🤖💻 — **Integrating machine learning and deep learning with multiple molecular fingerprints for topoisomerase I inhibitor screening.** Open activity-prediction model taking SMILES → TOP1 inhibition (classification), trained on ChEMBL across five fingerprint families and eight algorithms; the four best models reach ROC-AUC 0.93–0.94 and ship as standalone offline desktop applications. Plausible Hub addition because the fingerprint × algorithm sweep is exactly the shape the Hub packages well, though the repository carries no LICENSE file. · [code](https://github.com/zenghuang8006/TOP1-inhibitor-prediction) · 🎯

### 🧪 Property prediction

- [Macorano et al., *European Journal of Medicinal Chemistry*, 2026-09-07](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejmech.2026.119312) 🤖 — **XenoSoM: a deep learning framework for site of metabolism prediction of xenobiotics.** Open property model taking SMILES → per-atom site-of-metabolism probability, built on AttentiveFP in single-task and multi-task variants after benchmarking six graph architectures. Plausible Hub addition because it was trained on a curated corpus of more than 30,000 annotated substrates — an unusually large metabolism training set — and site-of-metabolism is an ADMET endpoint the Hub does not yet cover. · 🧪
- [Zhang et al., *Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling*, 2026-09-07](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.6c02770) 🤖 — **AquaTox-Predictor: an MMoE-enhanced multimodal deep learning framework for aquatic ecotoxicity prediction.** Open property model taking SMILES → seven aquatic ecotoxicity endpoints, fusing fingerprints and molecular graphs through a multi-gate mixture-of-experts head with substructure attributions. Plausible Hub addition because JCIM is the second-most-incorporated venue in the Hub catalogue and environmental toxicity complements the existing human-toxicity coverage. · 🧪
- [Huang et al., *JACS Au*, 2026-08-31](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacsau.6c01105) 🤖 — **MacroTox: a macroscopic graph topology-based multimodal learning framework for robust molecular toxicity prediction.** Open property model taking SMILES → toxicity labels, combining macroscopic graph topology with fingerprints and contextual sequence representations from East China Normal University. Plausible Hub addition because the authors report generalisation gains across multiple toxicity datasets, the failure mode that most often blocks Hub toxicity models — the article licence is CC-BY-NC-ND, so confirm the code terms first. · 🧪
- [Chen et al., *Journal of Computer-Aided Molecular Design*, 2026-09-02](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10822-026-00924-0) 🤖 — **PACL: property-aware contrastive learning with adaptive substructures for molecular property prediction.** Open property model taking SMILES → property predictions, using contrastive pretraining conditioned on the target property with substructures selected adaptively rather than by fixed fragmentation rules. Plausible Hub addition because property-aware pretraining transfers cheaply to the low-data antimicrobial endpoints where generic pretrained encoders underperform. · 🧪

### 🧩 Featurization

- [Zhou et al., *Communications Chemistry*, 2026-09-01](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42004-026-02167-4) 🤖 — **Geo-Hete-HyperGNN as a prior-free molecular hypergraph representation learning strategy.** Open featurization model taking SMILES plus 3D geometry → equivariant molecular embeddings, defining atom-type, bond-type, bond-angle and conjugated-system hyperedges without expert-crafted rules and reading out through a mixture of molecular hypergraph experts; released under CC-BY-4.0. Plausible Hub addition because featurization is a quarter of the Hub and a geometry-aware hypergraph encoder is a genuinely new representation family alongside the existing fingerprint and transformer embedders. · 🧩
- [Lin et al., *Chemical Science*, 2026-09-02](https://doi.org/10.1039/D6SC02982E) 🤖 — **Task-adaptive multimodal molecular representations for structure-sensitive property prediction.** Open featurization model taking SMILES → task-adapted multimodal embeddings, led from Macao Polytechnic University and released under CC-BY-3.0. Plausible Hub addition because the representation adapts per downstream task rather than being frozen, which is the practical weakness of the descriptor-style featurizers currently dominating the Hub. · 🧩
- [Xu et al., *Advanced Science*, 2026-09-01](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.77511) 🤖 — **Similarity-enhanced representation learning of non-canonical amino acids for therapeutic peptide modelling.** Open featurization model taking non-canonical amino acid structures → residue embeddings for peptide property models, released under CC-BY-4.0. Plausible Hub addition because non-canonical residues carry SMILES and are the blocker for extending Hub peptide models beyond the standard twenty — relevant to antimicrobial peptide work. · 🧩

### 🎨 Generative

- [Wang et al., *Communications Chemistry*, 2026-09-05](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42004-026-02188-z) 🤖 — **Controllable molecular generation with fine-tuned flow-matching model.** Open generative model taking property conditions → molecules, fine-tuning a flow-matching backbone for controllable generation, from the AstraZeneca molecular AI group and released under CC-BY-4.0. Plausible Hub addition because flow matching is faster to sample than the diffusion generators already in the Hub and the same group's earlier releases have packaged cleanly. · 🎨
- [Sun et al., *Molecular Diversity*, 2026-09-05](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11030-026-11718-9) 🤖 — **DFDM: a dynamic fusion diffusion model for molecular generation.** Open generative model taking noise → 2D molecular graphs, fusing a GINE spatial branch with a Chebyshev spectral branch under signal-to-noise-guided layer weights. Plausible Hub addition because it reports the lowest Fréchet ChemNet Distance and the highest uncorrected validity of the compared methods on QM9 and ZINC250k, and unconditional 2D generators are a thin part of the Hub. · 🎨

## 🗃️ Datasets that could join the Hub

### ADMET / property datasets

- [Durai et al., *Bioinformatics*, 2026-08-22](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btag617) 🗃️💻 — **ToxiVerse: chemical bioprofiling, toxicity data sharing and customizable predictive modelling.** ~50,000 curated chemicals · 50+ toxicological endpoints across ~1,600 PubChem assays · CC-BY · web platform plus GitHub. Plausible Hub input because it is the largest openly-licensed multi-endpoint toxicity collection to appear this quarter and its bioprofiling module gives ready-made negative sets, which single-endpoint toxicity corpora rarely provide. · [code](https://github.com/zhu-research-group/toxiverse) · 🧪
- [Shende & Tembhurne, *Preprints.org*, 2026-09-07](https://doi.org/10.20944/preprints202609.0517.v1) 🗃️ — **i-DILI: a multi-source database for drug-induced liver injury labelling and benchmarking.** 17,778 unique compounds (22.5 % positive) · drug-induced liver injury binary label plus a per-compound confidence score · valid SMILES · harmonised from LiverTox, DILIrank, DILIst, DrugBank, Open TG-GATEs and Tox21. Plausible Hub input because the confidence score makes the well-known label contradictions between DILI sources explicit rather than silently averaging them, which is precisely what a Hub-facing DILI model needs. · 🧪

### Featurization / multi-task benchmarks

- [Sun & Head-Gordon, *npj Drug Discovery*, 2026-09-02](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44386-026-00068-z) 🗃️ — **KinConfBench: a curated benchmark for cofolding models on kinase conformational states.** 2,225 high-quality human kinase chains · ligand-induced-fit conformational state recovery · evaluated against Boltz-2, Chai-1, Protenix and RoseTTAFold-All-Atom. Plausible Hub input because it shows that geometric pose metrics do not track the correct kinase conformational state — a direct caution for anyone reading Boltz-2 benchmark numbers — though at 2,225 chains it sits below the usual scale bar and is promoted on endpoint relevance. · 🗺️

## AI/ML methods for drug discovery

- [Cecchini et al., *Nature Biotechnology*, 2026-09-01](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-026-03217-x) ⭐ — **AI-enhanced adaptive virtual screening of large libraries for ligand discovery.** Forty-eight-author adaptive screening pipeline that reaches ultra-large library scale without exhaustive docking; the sampling strategy is the transferable part for open screening campaigns. (Input: receptor structure plus library — outside the Hub's small-molecule-only interface; licence CC-BY-NC-ND.)
- [Tang et al., *Nature*, 2026-09-09](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11025-1) ⭐ — **Breaking timescales with generative sampling of conformational transitions.** Generative sampler that reaches conformational transitions normally out of reach of molecular dynamics, of interest for target-state modelling on pathogen proteins. (Input: protein structure.)
- [Torren-Peraire et al., *Nature Communications*, 2026-09-02](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77324-3) — **Multi-objective optimization in the context of generative chemistry.** Systematic treatment of how objectives are defined, combined and evaluated in generative chemistry, from the Novartis side of the field; the clearest reference this week for judging the scoring functions inside any generative model proposed for incorporation.
- [Protopopov et al., *npj Drug Discovery*, 2026-09-02](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44386-026-00067-0) — **Pushing the boundaries of virtual screening scale of combinatorial spaces with the V-SYNTHES approach.** Synthon-based screening of combinatorial spaces far larger than any enumerable library, with synthesisability built into the search rather than filtered afterwards. (Input: receptor structure; licence CC-BY-NC-ND.)
- [Qin et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-06](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.02.748775) — **OmniSyn unifies target-aware molecular generation and optimization within a synthesis-native LLM framework across the human proteome.** Mixture-of-experts language model that emits molecules together with explicit synthesis traces, yielding a 2.7-billion-molecule target-specific virtual library. (Input: protein sequence; licence CC-BY-NC-ND and no code released — worth revisiting if the terms change.)
- [Wang et al., *WIREs Computational Molecular Science*, 2026-09](https://doi.org/10.1002/wcms.70084) — **Machine learning-aided small-molecule virtual screening: recent advances and future perspectives.** Industry-authored survey of where ML screening actually beats docking, organised by data regime rather than by architecture.
- [Simonetti et al., *Drug Discovery Today*, 2026-09](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.drudis.2026.104794) — **From chemical space to synthetic reality: a framework for makeable-by-design generative AI in drug discovery.** Argues that generative outputs judged on proxy metrics rather than synthesisability are not yet candidates, and proposes a makeability-first framing worth applying to Hub generative entries.
- [Suri et al., *RSC Advances*, 2026-09-08](https://doi.org/10.1039/d6ra05440d) 🌍 — **Cross-docking and redocking reveal distinct determinants of success in physics-based and AI-driven binding pose prediction.** IIIT Delhi comparison of AutoDock, Vina and DOCK 6 against GNINA 1.3, AlphaFold 3, Boltz-2 and DiffDock across three binding-site architectures, showing physics-based methods stay competitive on redocking but degrade under realistic cross-docking.
- [Ye, *In Silico Pharmacology*, 2026-09-06](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40203-026-00745-x) — **Can docking-score differences support 5-HT2A/5-HT2B subtype-selectivity decisions? An audited paired benchmark.** A clean negative result: across 419 paired activity records, Vina score differences correlate with experimental selectivity at r ≈ 0.10, below the pre-registered practical threshold. Directly relevant wherever docking deltas are used to argue selectivity.
- [Vu et al., *Computational and Structural Biotechnology Journal*, 2026-09-07](https://doi.org/10.34133/csbj.0217) — **A deep-learning-based scoring framework for large-scale multi-donor cardiotoxicity screening.** Autoencoder trained only on baseline signals scores 1,029 compounds across five donors by reconstruction error, removing the need for labels. (Input: calcium-transient recordings, not structures — but the label-free scoring pattern transfers to phenotypic assay readouts.)
- [Xiao et al., *Cell Reports Methods*, 2026-09-04](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crmeth.2026.101577) — **Deepeptide: a deep learning framework for oligopeptide candidate discovery across metabolism-related contexts.** Routes around indication-specific data scarcity by linking indication, biological process and molecular function, then validated across five disease contexts; released CC-BY. The scarcity workaround is the transferable idea for neglected-disease endpoints.

## Antibiotic and antimicrobial discovery

- [Asiki et al., *EMBO Molecular Medicine*, 2026-09-02](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44321-026-00515-4) — **New thinking for the next generation of antimalarials.** H3D-led perspective (Chibale group) on what should replace the current antimalarial discovery playbook, published CC-BY; the target and chemotype priorities set here are the ones open antimalarial models should be benchmarked against.
- [Kant et al., *ACS Infectious Diseases*, 2026-09-09](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsinfecdis.6c00521) 🌍 — **Artificial intelligence-integrated proteome-wide target prioritization for drug discovery against multidrug-resistant *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*.** Shoolini University and Southampton pipeline ranking the whole gonococcal proteome for druggability, released CC-BY. (Input: proteome, not molecules.) Gonorrhoea is a WHO priority pathogen with almost no open computational tooling.
- [Kumar et al., *Bioorganic Chemistry*, 2026-09-08](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bioorg.2026.110483) 🌍 — **Computational small molecule drug discovery for multidrug-resistant tuberculosis: emerging targets and recent medicinal chemistry.** India-led survey of the MDR- and XDR-TB target landscape and the chemotypes moving against it; a useful checklist of which targets an open TB activity model could plausibly cover.
- [Li et al., *Spectrochimica Acta A*, 2026-09-07](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.saa.2026.128745) — **Single-cell Raman spectroscopy combined with deep learning for antimicrobial resistance detection in *Mycobacterium abscessus*.** Deuterium-labelled single-cell Raman plus a CNN discriminates clarithromycin- and linezolid-resistant cells in 24 hours without culture — a culture-free susceptibility route for a pathogen where conventional testing is slowest.
- [Li et al., *Environmental Pollution*, 2026-09-04](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envpol.2026.129105) — **Global freshwater resistomes reveal environmental signatures associated with the burden of drug-resistant tuberculosis.** 1,280 freshwater metagenomes across 45 countries yield an environmental AMR Risk Score that explains variation in MDR-TB burden beyond socio-economic factors, with income-stratified analyses.
- [Zhou et al., *Naunyn-Schmiedeberg's Archives of Pharmacology*, 2026-09-09](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00210-026-05878-6) — **Berberine inhibits the AcrB efflux pump and synergizes with meropenem against carbapenem-resistant hypervirulent *Klebsiella pneumoniae*.** Efflux-pump potentiation of an existing carbapenem, in the adjuvant direction that Gram-negative programmes keep returning to.
- [Sousa et al., *Journal of Molecular Modeling*, 2026-09-09](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00894-026-06923-0) — **Molecular modeling design of antitrypanosomal pyrazolone derivatives targeting Chagas disease.** Brazil-led QSAR, docking and dynamics on a pyrazolone series against *Trypanosoma cruzi*; Chagas remains one of the thinnest areas of open activity data.
- [Paul et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-08](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.04.749322) 🌍 — **Integrated in silico identification of fungal-derived dual-target inhibitors for anti-schistosomal drug discovery.** Bangladesh-led screen of 1,831 fungal metabolites against *Schistosoma mansoni* DHODH and cathepsin B1, with decoy-validated docking (AUC 0.96–0.98). Schistosomiasis dual-target chemistry is largely absent from open collections.
- [Zahid & Riaz, *Research Square*, 2026-09-08](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10927781/v1) — **Confidence-graded, proteome-wide identification of candidate antimicrobial peptide precursors in the Ghaf tree (*Prosopis cineraria*).** Pre-registered survey of 29,222 proteins that reports a classifier background positive rate and calibrated significance — the methodological discipline most proteome AMP-mining papers omit.

## AI agents and foundation models for science

- [Sun et al., *Nature*, 2026-09-09](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11001-9) ⭐ — **An operational perturbation proteomics-based virtual cell model.** ProteinTalks is pretrained on more than 38 million temporal protein-abundance measurements from perturbed cell lines and predicts drug effects from learned dynamical latent representations; the clearest demonstration yet that virtual-cell models need time-resolved perturbation data rather than more static omics.
- [Liu & Wang, *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-08](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.03.749148) 💻 — **bioq: a unified, agent-native command-line interface to a fleet of AI drug-discovery methods.** MIT-licensed CLI over 38+ containerised tools spanning seven discovery stages and six modalities, run on serverless GPUs so no local CUDA stack is needed — the closest external analogue to what the Ersilia Model Hub CLI does, and worth reading for its self-describing interface design and its answer to the hardware-access problem. · [code](https://github.com/wolfsonliu/bioq)
- [Soman et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-09](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.04.749404) — **Novel target combinations in lung squamous cell carcinoma proposed by the Emet AI research environment.** An agentic research director drives graph-of-thoughts search over a 1.5-billion-triple biomedical knowledge graph to generate target-combination hypotheses; the hypothesis-then-verify loop is the transferable pattern for neglected-disease target work.

## Global health and open science

- [Baleba et al., *Journal of Insect Science*, 2026-09-01](https://doi.org/10.1093/jisesa/ieag087) 🌍 — **Machine learning-enabled chemical ecology for integrated pest management: from volatiles to field applications.** Led from icipe in Nairobi and released CC-BY, this traces generative and predictive chemistry from semiochemical discovery through to field deployment — African-led applied cheminformatics rather than work about Africa, and directly adjacent to vector-control chemistry.
- [Mangal et al., *Journal of Molecular Graphics and Modelling*, 2026-09-04](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmgm.2026.109566) 🌍 — **SilicoXplore: an integrated cloud platform coupling machine learning with physics-based modelling for end-to-end drug discovery.** India-led attempt to give labs without local compute an end-to-end pipeline; the cloud-only delivery is the opposite of the containerised, run-anywhere model the Hub uses, which makes the trade-off worth examining.
- [Amin & Sharna, *Integrative Biomedical Research*, 2026-09-01](https://doi.org/10.25163/biomedical.10110890) 🌍 — **From days to hours: artificial intelligence in antimicrobial resistance diagnostics and drug discovery, and why no tool has yet reached the clinic.** Bangladesh-led account of the translation gap between published AMR models and clinical deployment; the failure modes it lists are the ones open AMR tooling has to answer.

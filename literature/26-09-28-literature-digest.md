## 🤖 Models that could join the Hub

### 🎯 Activity prediction

- [Kim et al., *J Chem Inf Model*, 2026-09-17](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.6c01761) 🤖 — **CUE: a chemical uncertainty-aware embedding framework for multimodal drug selectivity prediction.** Open activity-prediction model taking SMILES → a compound-level selectivity score, fusing fingerprints with 2D structure-image embeddings and reweighting each modality by its measured reliability; licence and repository are not named in the abstract, so confirm both before packaging. Plausible Hub addition because selectivity is currently inferred by chaining several affinity models, and the accumulated error is exactly what a direct predictor removes. · 🎯
- [Zhang et al., *npj Digit Med*, 2026-09-17](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-026-03193-1) 🤖 — **Probabilistic multimodal learning for drug–target interaction prediction.** Compound–protein interaction model taking SELFIES plus conformational information and a protein structure representation → an interaction probability with a calibrated uncertainty estimate. Plausible Hub addition because the molecule stays the user-facing input, and a DTI predictor that reports its own confidence is more useful for triage than a bare score. · 🎯
- [Bedoui et al., *Pharmaceuticals*, 2026-09-14](https://doi.org/10.3390/ph19091458) 🤖 — **KAN-GIN: adaptive nonlinear molecular representation learning for drug–target affinity prediction.** Affinity model taking a molecular graph plus a convolutional protein-sequence encoding → a DTA value, swapping the usual post-aggregation MLP for a Kolmogorov–Arnold layer and benchmarked on DAVIS, KIBA and METZ under a matched protocol. Plausible Hub addition because it is a drop-in architectural change to the GIN encoders several Hub activity models already rely on. · 🎯
- [Abbasi et al., *PLoS One*, 2026-09-22](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0357278) 🤖 — **RA-PLA: retrieval-augmented graph convolutional networks for protein–ligand binding affinity prediction.** Affinity model taking SMILES plus a protein representation → a binding-affinity score, retrieving conflicting-label neighbours at inference and fine-tuning per query. Reported gains are largest in the cold-target split, which is the regime every neglected-disease endpoint sits in. Plausible Hub addition for exactly that reason. · 🎯

### 🧪 Property prediction

- [Huang et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-25](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.21.753060) 🤖 — **MetExPred: protein-context-aware multi-view learning for drug metabolism and excretion.** Property model taking SMILES → 17 classification endpoints plus clearance and half-life regression, with an optional protein channel that activates only when an annotated target is available `(weights: pending)`. Plausible Hub addition because metabolism and excretion are the thinnest part of the Hub's ADMET coverage, and one entry would fill 19 endpoints at once. · 🧪
- [He et al., *Commun Chem*, 2026-09-24](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42004-026-02194-1) 🤖 — **MEGPNM: a multiscale edge-aware GAT with hybrid pooling for predicting permeability of non-peptidic macrocycles.** Property model taking SMILES → passive permeability for macrocyclic chemotypes, a class where the standard descriptor-based rules break down. Plausible Hub addition because macrocycles keep appearing in antimicrobial series and no Hub permeability model currently covers them. · 🧪
- [Barajas et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-18](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.16.751426) 🤖 — **Machine learning for toxicity prediction in low-sample molecular classes.** Property model taking SMILES → probabilistic toxicity calls, fine-tuning a Chemprop-style baseline on small class-specific sets (organophosphates, androgen- and estrogen-receptor antagonists) and beating both classical baselines and the EPA TEST tool. Plausible Hub addition because the transfer recipe, not just the weights, is what neglected-disease endpoints with a few hundred labels need. · 🧪
- [Dinan, *GitHub*, 2026-08-17](https://github.com/adamd3/perm-predict) 🤖💻 — **Perm-Predict.** Property model taking SMILES → a bacterial compound-accumulation score, staged as a near-zero-accumulation classifier followed by an XGBoost / AttentiveFP / DimeNet++ regression ensemble, released under MIT with featurisation and inference code in the repository. Plausible Hub addition because Gram-negative accumulation is the bottleneck the `E-AMR-CC` track is built around and the licence is already compatible. · [code](https://github.com/adamd3/perm-predict) · 🧪

### 🧩 Featurization

- [Kumar et al., *Nat Commun*, 2026-09-19](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77700-z) 🤖 — **Scalable molecular representations enabled by multimodal fusion and sequence distillation.** Featurizer taking SMILES → a unified embedding distilled from six views (physicochemical, topological, 2D image, bioactivity profile, quantum and molecular language), so the fused representation is recoverable from sequence alone at inference. Plausible Hub addition because it collapses several descriptor families that the Hub currently exposes as separate entries into one featurization call. · 🧩
- [Hamada et al., *Nat Comput Sci*, 2026-09-17](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43588-026-01036-3) 🤖 — **Revisiting molecular descriptors with TDiMS for interpretable intramolecular interactions based on substructure pairs.** Featurizer taking SMILES → a descriptor vector that enumerates pairwise topological distances between substructures, capturing the long-range intramolecular interactions that local fingerprints discard, and staying human-readable. Plausible Hub addition because interpretable descriptors are what let a modeller explain a prediction to a medicinal chemist, and the Hub is short of them. · 🧩
- [Qiao et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-21](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.15.751932) 🤖 — **LARK: reaction-aware hypergraph learning of chemical representations.** Featurizer taking SMILES → pretrained embeddings learned from ~0.6 M reactions rather than isolated structures, transferring to 42 downstream tasks spanning reaction outcomes, physicochemistry, toxicity, metabolism and pharmacokinetics `(weights: pending)`. Plausible Hub addition because a single encoder covering both reaction and single-molecule property tasks is unusually broad for one model entry. · 🧩

### 🎨 Generative

- [Li et al., *Nat Mach Intell*, 2026-09-23](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-026-01304-x) ⭐🤖 — **Guiding large language models to predict edit sequences for molecular synthesizability optimization.** Generative model taking SMILES → an ordered sequence of structural edits that moves a molecule toward synthetic accessibility, rather than re-sampling a whole new structure. Plausible Hub addition because every Hub generator emits compounds nobody has checked for makeability, and an edit-sequence model is the repair step rather than another filter. · 🎨
- [Grebner et al., *Commun Chem*, 2026-09-15](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42004-026-02193-2) 🤖 — **Exploration and validation of large language models as tools for molecular optimization.** Generative system taking SMILES plus a natural-language objective → optimised analogues, validated against independent property models built on different descriptor sets so that a candidate must satisfy several predictors rather than the one it was optimised against. Plausible Hub addition because the multi-model validation loop is reusable independently of the generator itself. · 🎨
- [Zhang et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-23](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.17.752345) 🤖 — **REAL-SWIT: generative access to make-on-demand chemical space enables ultra-large virtual screening.** Generative model emitting whole molecules drawn from a learned Enamine REAL Space distribution — roughly 96 % of generated structures were found in REAL Space — coupled to a target-specific scorer, with six confirmed ROCK1 inhibitors from 23 compounds synthesised. Plausible Hub addition because it generates purchasable molecules by construction, which is the property that makes a generator usable by a partner lab. · 🎨
- [Xie et al., *Nat Commun*, 2025-03-10](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-56349-0) 🤖💻 — **TransPharmer: pharmacophore-informed generative models for bioactive ligand discovery.** Generative model taking a topological pharmacophore fingerprint → novel SMILES that preserve the pharmacophore while varying scaffold, released with inference code and checkpoints under MIT and already applied to design low-nanomolar selective kinase inhibitors. Plausible Hub addition because the licence and packaging are already compatible and pharmacophore-conditioned generation needs no protein structure. · [code](https://github.com/iipharma/transpharmer-repo) · 🎨
- [Moon et al., *chemRxiv*, 2025-12-18](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv-2025-x5wt5) 🤖 — **R²GB-GA: a reaction-regulated graph-based genetic algorithm for exploring synthesizable chemical space.** Generative model evolving SMILES under reaction-rule constraints so that every proposed structure carries an implied synthetic route, from the Gwangju Institute of Science and Technology. Plausible Hub addition because a genetic algorithm has no weights to host and no GPU requirement, which makes it one of the cheapest generators to containerise. · 🎨

### 🔍 Similarity

- [Chen et al., *Research Square*, 2026-09-23](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-11130410/v1) 🤖 — **SCOPE: from shape and colour to binding affinity, with confidence.** Ligand-based model taking a 3D conformer → an affinity estimate with an applicability-domain confidence weight, ensembling shape-and-colour overlays with kernel PLS and Gaussian-process regression and beating a 2D baseline under cluster-based cross-validation. Plausible Hub addition because the target-agnostic confidence mapping is transferable to Hub models that currently return an unqualified number. · 🔍

## 🗃️ Datasets that could join the Hub

### Bioactivity datasets

- [Kang et al., *J Chem Inf Model*, 2026-09-15](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.6c02102) 🗃️ — **An AI-driven chemogenomics knowledgebase for human-transmissible pathogens (HPM-CKB).** 234,287 bioactive molecules against 7,876 pathogenic proteins from 267 microorganisms, with 13,914 associated 3D structures · pathogen bioactivity · licence not stated on the release page · web platform from the Third Military Medical University. Plausible Hub input because a pathogen-scoped bioactivity corpus at this scale is the single largest training resource for antimicrobial activity models to appear this year. · 🎯
- [Wang and Cheng, *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-23](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.17.752433) 🗃️ — **PLABench: leakage-controlled benchmarking reveals generalization limits of deep learning for protein–ligand binding affinity prediction.** Leakage-controlled splits over ChEMBL35, CASP16 blind targets, Davis and KIBA · binding affinity · released as an extensible open-source platform · University of Missouri. Plausible Hub input because the finding that structural fidelity buys no consistent gain is a direct warning for how Hub affinity models are evaluated. · 🎯

### Featurization / multi-task benchmarks

- [Alwer et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-28](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.25.751968) 🗃️ — **Accessing enzyme kinetic data and prediction methods at scale (OpenKinetics).** CatLog, a curated enzyme-kinetics dataset shipped with precomputed embeddings, predicted binding sites and standardised splits, alongside thirteen prediction methods behind one open-source interface · kinetic parameters · University of Galway. Plausible Hub input because the standardised splits and embeddings remove most of the setup cost for anyone benchmarking substrate-level models. · 🧩
- [Lu et al., *npj Comput Mater*, 2026-09-21](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-026-02315-3) 🗃️ — **OMC-bench and AtomBit-OMC: task-aligned benchmarks and interpretable machine-learned interatomic potentials for organic molecular crystals.** Task-aligned benchmark suite over organic molecular crystals with accompanying interatomic potentials · solid-state stability and polymorph ranking · released as a benchmark plus model pair. Plausible Hub input because crystal-form stability is the property that decides whether a small-molecule lead is formulable, and nothing in the Hub currently touches it. · 🧪

## AI/ML methods for drug discovery

- [Xu et al., *Nature*, 2026-09-16](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11013-5) ⭐ — **Development of a random background to understand ligand optimization.** Establishes what a *random* small substitution actually achieves, giving lead-optimisation campaigns a null model to measure against; the answer reframes how much of reported medicinal-chemistry progress is signal. Any benchmark of a generative or optimisation model should be reading this before claiming an improvement.
- [Halip et al., *Nat Rev Drug Discov*, 2026-09-21](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41573-026-01530-3) ⭐ — **The evolving landscape of drug targets.** A refreshed census of what is actually druggable and by what modality, from the group that maintains the field's target-tractability resources. Useful as the reference frame when deciding which pathogen targets are worth a Hub activity model at all.
- [Torren-Peraire et al., *Nat Commun*, 2026-09-02](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77324-3) — **Multi-objective optimization in the context of generative chemistry.** Systematic treatment of how competing objectives should be combined when a generator is scored on potency, drug-likeness and synthesisability at once — the decision the Hub currently makes implicitly by chaining separate models.
- [Igashov et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-18](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.15.751537) — **LDDM: a unified 3D generative model for synthesizable structure-based drug design.** One framework covering constrained and unconstrained docking, fragment linking and growing, with large-scale experimental validation, from EPFL. (Input: protein pocket — Hub-eligible only once pocket conditioning is supported.)
- [Utgés et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-18](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.16.752029) — **Interrogating contrastive learning embeddings for structure-based virtual screening: a case study on DrugCLIP.** Dissects what a shared pocket–ligand embedding space actually encodes, finding the pocket embeddings set a new state of the art in pocket similarity search while running over 100 times faster. (Input: protein pocket.) A useful caution for anyone reading too much chemistry into a learned latent space.
- [Araripe et al., *J Chem Inf Model*, 2026-09-23](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.6c00802) — **CAPRICHO: interpretable quality flagging and flexible ChEMBL bioactivity curation for QSAR modelling.** A Python command-line tool from Leiden that flags questionable ChEMBL records rather than silently dropping them, letting each curation decision be inspected for its effect on cross-assay comparability. Directly relevant to how Hub training sets are assembled from ChEMBL.
- [Jansen et al., *J Chem Inf Model*, 2026-09-16](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.6c01654) — **The Scope concept: defined chemical space for identifying ligand-efficient, tractable hits.** Defines a screening space between fragments and fully elaborated leads, exemplified on trypanosomal CLK1 among other targets, with ring-X-ring systems as the organising principle for library design. A concrete recipe for building a 10k–50k screening set for a neglected-disease target.

## Antibiotic and antimicrobial discovery

- [Gray et al., *Nat Chem Biol*, 2026-09-16](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41589-026-02319-6) ⭐ — **Small-molecule inhibitors block NorA efflux by conformational trapping.** IMP-2380 restores ciprofloxacin susceptibility in MRSA with low-nanomolar potentiation and in vivo efficacy, with a 2.52-Å cryo-EM structure of the inhibitor-bound pump. Efflux potentiation is the mechanism most likely to rescue existing antibiotics, and this is the first selective chemical probe with a structure to match.
- [Soley et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-19](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.18.752562) — **A panel of genotypically and phenotypically characterised WHO priority Gram-negative bacteria.** 145 UKHSA reference isolates across 81 sequence types spanning *E. coli*, *K. pneumoniae*, carbapenem-resistant *A. baumannii* and *P. aeruginosa*, each with susceptibility data and whole-genome sequences. The kind of shared reference panel that makes antimicrobial results comparable between labs.
- [Meng et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-24](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.21.753226) — **Discovery and optimisation of imidazothiazole carboxamides as novel anti-tuberculosis agents.** A hit from the open-access CRESTdb library optimised to analogues active against MDR and XDR clinical isolates with 62 % oral bioavailability in mouse. A structurally differentiated antitubercular chemotype that came out of an open library, which is the pipeline shape worth repeating.
- [Kaufmann et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-21](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.20.752996) — **3-Hydroxypropanamidines: from antiplasmodial leads to a novel antibacterial scaffold.** Antimalarial arylamino-alcohol relatives repurposed against MRSA, VISA and several Gram-negative species, with proteomic response profiling and honest reporting of narrow selectivity indices. A worked example of antimalarial chemistry crossing into antibacterial space.
- [Exertier et al., *ACS Omega*, 2026-09-08](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.6c05158) — **UbC4 from *Leishmania* is a druggable E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme.** Structural basis plus fragment hits suggesting that in *Leishmania* the E2 enzyme, rather than an E3 ligase, is the handle a PROTAC should engage — the classical VHL route being unavailable. Opens targeted degradation as a modality for a neglected disease where resistance and toxicity limit current drugs.

## AI agents and foundation models for science

- [Noori et al., *Cell*, 2026-09](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.032) ⭐ — **World models for biomedicine.** Argues for models that simulate how biological systems respond to perturbation rather than predicting static labels, from the group behind the biomedical AI-agents line of work. The framing matters for anyone deciding whether the next Hub entry should be a predictor or a simulator.
- [Shi et al., *Nat Mach Intell*, 2026-09-10](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-026-01298-6) ⭐ — **A collaborative agent with two lightweight synergistic models for autonomous crystal materials research.** Closed-loop autonomous research agent built from small specialised models rather than one large generalist, which is the architecture that stays affordable outside well-funded labs.
- [Vangala et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-18](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.12.751167) — **TRACEDD: tool-grounded reasoning and agentic coordination for explainable drug design.** A tool-first multi-agent architecture where language models orchestrate validated computational tools instead of replacing them, running target validation through to retrosynthesis in a Reason–Act–Observe loop. The tool-first posture is the design decision that makes agentic pipelines auditable.
- [Cui et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-24](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.19.752921) — **A closed-loop language-model agent for target-specific multi-objective hit-to-lead optimization.** Benchmarked over six kinase targets with a fixed 40-candidate budget, and honest that re-scoring the same candidates under different evaluation rules changes which method wins. The evaluation-sensitivity result is the transferable finding, not the agent.
- [Nguyen et al., *arXiv*, 2026-09-18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21165) — **SpecOpt: contact-diff reasoning for agentic molecule optimization toward binding specificity.** Frames selectivity improvement of an existing drug as a constrained edit task rather than de novo design, preserving structural identity while shifting the on- versus off-target preference. Closer to how a medicinal chemist actually works than most generative formulations.

## Global health and open science

- [Ingelbeen et al., *Lancet Infect Dis*, 2026-10](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1473-3099(26)00169-6) — **Effect of a community-based behavioural intervention bundle to improve antibiotic use in Burkina Faso and DR Congo (CABU-EICO).** A cluster-randomised trial of community education plus provider feedback built on WHO AWaRe guidance, measuring Watch-group antibiotic use where most stewardship evidence is hospital-based and high-income. Stewardship at the community dispenser is where sub-Saharan AMR pressure is actually generated.
- [Ghimire et al., *BMC Public Health*, 2026-09-25](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-026-29488-2) 🌍 — **Policy intervention for antimicrobial resistance mitigation in Nepal: a scoping review of statutory and policy instruments.** Nepali-led audit of which AMR instruments exist on paper and which have enforcement behind them. National-level policy mapping of this kind is what turns AMR surveillance data into action.
- [Geta et al., *BMC Vet Res*, 2026-09-24](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12917-026-05938-2) 🌍 — **Prevalence and antimicrobial resistance profiles of bacteria isolated from fish in Ethiopia: a systematic review and meta-analysis.** Ethiopian-led synthesis of resistance in aquaculture and wild fish, a One Health reservoir that is routinely excluded from AMR burden estimates.
- [Vors et al., *J Glob Antimicrob Resist*, 2026-09](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jgar.2026.09.005) — **Assessment of the antimicrobial resistance research ecosystem in the Caribbean region.** Bibliometric mapping of AMR output across ten CARICOM low- and middle-income countries from 2010 to 2025, documenting where capacity is concentrated and where it is absent. Worth noting that a study of research capacity in ten LMICs is itself led from a high-income institution — the gap the paper measures is visible in its own author list.
- [Bagcchi, *Lancet Infect Dis*, 2026-10](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1473-3099(26)00473-1) — **WHO updates guidelines for visceral leishmaniasis.** Revised treatment recommendations for a disease whose drug options remain toxic, long and largely unchanged for decades. Sets the clinical bar any new antileishmanial candidate has to clear.

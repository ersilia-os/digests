## 🤖 Models that could join the Hub

### 🎯 Activity prediction

- [Pan et al., *J Chem Inf Model*, 2026-09-10](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.6c02659) 🤖 — **A task-adaptive multimodal pretrained framework for antibiotic virtual screening with joint activity and cytotoxicity prediction.** Open-source activity-prediction model taking SMILES (MACCS fingerprints plus a pretrained multimodal encoder) → paired antibacterial-activity and cytotoxicity scores; the pretraining step is what cuts the labelled-data requirement. Released code and licence are not stated in the abstract, so confirm both before packaging. Plausible Hub addition because it predicts the therapeutic-window trade-off in one pass rather than requiring two separate models, which is exactly the shape of the triage the `E-AMR-CC` track needs. · 🎯
- [Fitch et al., *Adv Sci*, 2026-09-16](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.76959) 🤖 — **Artificial-intelligence-guided phenotypic drug repurposing against *Streptococcus pneumoniae*.** Activity-prediction model from Imperial College London taking SMILES → predicted phenotypic antipneumococcal activity, used to rank an approved-drug library for repurposing; the chemRxiv preprint has been public since February, so the code trail is older than the journal version. Plausible Hub addition because repurposing-oriented whole-cell predictors on a WHO-priority respiratory pathogen are a standing Hub gap. · 🎯
- [Wang et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-15](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.12.751098v1) 🤖 — **Deep reinforcement learning-driven discovery of an MsbA-targeted small-molecule antibiotic for *Acinetobacter baumannii*.** Reinforcement-learning generator plus activity scorer taking SMILES → optimised MsbA-directed candidates, carried through to an in vivo *A. baumannii* infection model. Weights and licence not declared in the preprint. Plausible Hub addition because MsbA sits on the lipopolysaccharide pathway that the Gram-negative accumulation work already targets, and a validated generator-plus-scorer pair is more useful than either half alone. · 🎯

### 🧪 Property prediction

- [Zhang et al., *J Chem Inf Model*, 2026-09-08](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.6c02529) 🤖 — **SynOmega: simplifying retrosynthesis for efficient synthesizability scoring.** Property-calculation model taking SMILES → a scalar synthesizability score, derived from a simplified retrosynthesis expansion rather than a full route search, which is what makes it cheap enough to run over a library. Licence not stated. Plausible Hub addition because every generative model in the Hub emits molecules nobody has scored for makeability, and a fast SMILES-in/score-out synthesizability filter is the missing downstream step. · 🧪
- [Cui et al., *Pharmaceuticals*, 2026-09-14](https://doi.org/10.3390/ph19091453) 🤖 — **Multi-endpoint prediction of bioactivity and ADMET properties coupled with multi-objective optimisation in molecular descriptor space.** Property model taking descriptor vectors derived from SMILES → pIC50 plus five binary ADMET endpoints, trained on 1,974 compounds with 729 descriptors and wrapped in a multi-objective optimiser. The training set is small and the target is oncological rather than anti-infective. Plausible Hub addition mainly as a pattern: the joint potency-plus-ADMET objective is the decision the Hub currently makes by chaining separate models. · 🧪

### 🧩 Featurization

- [Guo et al., *J Chem Inf Model*, 2026-09-11](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.6c01681) 🤖 — **UniMolRep: a Python package for AI-oriented molecular representation modelling.** Featurizer taking SMILES → representation vectors across several pretrained backbones behind one interface, benchmarked on property, toxicity and quantum tasks and positioned explicitly against RDKit and DeepChem. It ships as an installable package, so code exists; the licence is not named in the abstract. Plausible Hub addition because a single wrapper exposing several encoders is close to the Hub's own featurization contract, and it would let one model entry cover several descriptor families. · 🧩
- [Yun et al., *arXiv*, 2026-09-14](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15611) 🤖 — **HiFi-Mol: multi-view molecular representation learning with hierarchical graphs and contextualised fingerprints.** Featurizer taking SMILES → pretrained embeddings from two separately pretrained encoders (hierarchical graph and contextualised fingerprint) fused at fine-tuning time; the stated motivation is generalising to structurally novel compounds from few labels. No weights or repository named in the preprint, so treat as `(weights: pending)`. Plausible Hub addition because low-label generalisation is the binding constraint on every neglected-disease endpoint the Hub covers. · 🧩

### 🎨 Generative

- [Irwin et al., *arXiv*, 2026-09-14](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15077) 🤖 — **Ensemble-conditioned molecular design.** Generative model from AstraZeneca emitting 3D structures conditioned on properties of the whole conformational distribution rather than a single assumed bioactive pose, and extended to flexible-size generation. No weights or licence declared yet `(weights: pending)`. Plausible Hub addition because it generates small molecules without requiring a pocket tensor, which is the condition that gates most 3D generators out of the Hub's generator interface. · 🎨

### 🗺️ Projection

- [Surendran et al., *Chem Sci*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1039/d6sc03716j) 🤖 — **Efficient visualisation of chemical space.** Projection method from the University of Florida taking molecular representations → low-dimensional embeddings of large compound collections, aimed at making chemical-space layout tractable at library scale. Licence and code availability not confirmed. Plausible Hub addition because projection is the thinnest bucket in the Hub at 3 % of Ready models, and library-scale layout is the step that makes an `E-AMR-CC` style curated collection navigable rather than just downloadable. · 🗺️

## 🗃️ Datasets that could join the Hub

### Bioactivity datasets

- [Kang et al., *J Chem Inf Model*, 2026-09-15](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.6c02102) 🗃️ — **An AI-driven chemogenomics knowledgebase for human-transmissible pathogens.** 234,287 bioactive molecules against 7,876 proteins from 267 pathogens, plus 13,914 protein structures · pathogen bioactivity · licence not declared · web resource at `cgai.asia/g/pathogenDB`. The authors validate it end-to-end, recovering SARS-CoV-2 repurposing hits and two novel-scaffold anti-*Staphylococcus aureus* leads against FtsZ. Plausible Hub input because it is the broadest pathogen-indexed bioactivity table released this year and covers WHO-priority species the Hub has no training data for — but the missing licence, not the data, is the gating step. · 🎯

## AI/ML methods for drug discovery

- [Xu et al., *Nature*, 2026-09-16](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11013-5) ⭐ — **Development of a random background to understand ligand optimisation.** Builds an explicit null distribution for what ligand optimisation achieves by chance, giving a baseline against which reported potency gains can be judged. The most useful reading this week for anyone assessing a model's claimed enrichment.
- [Qian et al., *J Chem Inf Model*, 2026-09-14](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.6c01275) — **MoaNet: learning protein–ligand mechanism of action from transcriptional profiles and sequence information.** Outperforms molecular-representation baselines adapted to mechanism-of-action prediction `(input: transcriptional profile + protein sequence)`, so it sits outside the Hub's small-molecule-input surface despite the chemistry framing.
- [Gangwal & Lavecchia, *Drug Discov Today*, 2026-09-14](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.drudis.2026.104804) 🌍 — **From implicit prioritisation to auditable decisions in natural-product drug discovery.** India-led perspective arguing that metabolomics and cheminformatics capacity has outrun decision discipline, and proposing a framework that records how evidence becomes an experimental action. Directly relevant to how Hub predictions get justified downstream.
- [Jang et al., *arXiv*, 2026-09-14](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15552) — **Synthesising state-of-the-art structure predictions from a soup of co-folding models.** Shows independently trained co-folding models encode complementary information that transfers between them, so ensembling beats any single model `(input: protein sequence)`. Worth reading alongside the federated co-folding result below.
- [Rieger et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-11](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.09.750355v1) — **Reframing enzyme function prediction as conditional generation.** Argues that classification framings cannot discover unseen chemistry and recasts promiscuous-activity discovery as generation `(input: protein sequence)`. The framing argument transfers to bioactivity prediction on endpoints with no positive examples.
- [Moore, *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-17](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.14.744956v2) 💻 — **Lacuna: cryptic binding pocket discovery via conformational ensemble analysis.** Open-source Python tool generating a conformational ensemble from any input structure to find pockets invisible in the unbound form `(input: protein structure)`. Relevant to the binding-site-comparison work on Clp unfoldases and peptidases.
- [Li et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-14](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.07.749983v1) — **ForceFlowAb: physics-aware mixture-of-experts flow matching for antibody CDR design.** Antigen-conditioned CDR co-design addressing local interface heterogeneity `(input: antigen structure)`.
- [Goodman, *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-17](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.07.743632v4) — **Collateral-sensitivity strongly connected components in real-world clinical surveillance data are confounded by clonal lineage.** A clean negative result: the cyclic collateral-sensitivity structures that would license drug-cycling strategies dissolve into lineage structure once clonality is accounted for. Read before trusting collateral sensitivity as a modelling target.
- [Jha et al., *arXiv*, 2026-09-10](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11917) — **Data scarcity and model sparsity: mixtures-of-experts overfit more to repeated data.** Sparse architectures degrade faster than dense ones when training data is repeated — a direct caution for the mixture-of-experts designs now common in molecular property prediction, where chemical data is repeated far more aggressively than text.
- [Xuan et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-11](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.28.747922v2) — **Corpusome, a cross-body-site human microbiome corpus for representation learning.** Harmonised multi-body-site corpus carrying the technical metadata needed to model batch structure rather than ignore it `(input: microbiome profile)`. The batch-aware curation pattern is the transferable part.
- [OpenADMET, 2026-09-17](https://openadmet.ghost.io/applicability-domain-how-to-know-when-your-model-is-actually-relevant/) — **Applicability domain: how to know when your model is actually relevant.** Practitioner write-up on deciding when a trained predictor should decline to answer. The question every Hub model currently answers implicitly by returning a number regardless of input.

## Antibiotic and antimicrobial discovery

- [Geher et al., *eLife*, 2026-09-14](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.111494) — **Acyl carrier protein is essential for apicoplast biogenesis in malaria parasites independent of fatty acid synthesis.** Separates ACP's essential role from the FASII pathway it was assumed to serve, resolving long-standing confusion about apicoplast fatty-acid biosynthesis as an antimalarial target and pointing at a different vulnerability.
- [Mendes et al., *npj Drug Discov*, 2026-09-16](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44386-026-00073-2) — **Harnessing snake venom cardiotoxins for antimicrobial peptide discovery.** Two computational toxin-mining strategies extract encrypted antimicrobial peptides from cobra cardiotoxins. Peptide output, so outside the Hub's input surface, but the mining-an-existing-proteome pattern is reusable.
- [Moulding et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-17](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.10.18.618978v3) — **Broad-spectrum bacterial polyamine detoxification inhibitors as antivirulence agents and antibiotic adjuvants.** Targets the host-polyamine detoxification that pathogens use to survive at infection sites — an adjuvant strategy rather than a growth-inhibition one, which changes what a whole-cell activity model should be trained to predict.
- [Spieles et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-16](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.15.751812v1) — **Inhibitors of spore germination act against diverse human fungal pathogens.** Screens 191 molecules against a process conserved across critical fungal pathogens but absent in mammalian cells, which is a principled route around the host-toxicity problem that limits current antifungals.
- [Van Overfelt et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-16](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.14.751387v1) — **The Mosigvirus AV110 elongasome-modulating peptide inhibits *Escherichia coli* growth by targeting Mre-dependent peptidoglycan synthesis.** Mines early phage proteins for new antibacterial targets against ESBL- and AmpC-producing *E. coli*.
- [Connor et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-17](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.24.666686v2) — **Benchmarking tools for resolving the plasmidome from short-read assemblies for *Klebsiella pneumoniae*.** Evaluates which plasmid-reconstruction tools survive contact with the short-read data that clinical and public-health labs actually generate — the practical constraint on AMR surveillance in low-resource settings.
- [Olejniczak et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-09-17](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.11.750917v1) — **DepoCat: interactive database of experimentally verified phage depolymerases.** Curates experimentally confirmed *Klebsiella* phage depolymerases against sequence repositories dominated by unverified computational annotation. A useful reminder of how much annotation noise sits inside training data drawn from public repositories.

## AI agents and foundation models for science

- [Miao et al., *Nature*, 2026-09-16](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11044-y) ⭐ — **Reimagining research papers as interactive and reliable AI agents.** Proposes publishing papers as queryable agents rather than static documents. The reliability half is the interesting half, and the idea maps closely onto what a model registry with executable entries already is.
- [Zhou et al., *arXiv*, 2026-09-09](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10121) — **ADMET-EvO: a self-evolving scientific agent for sustained research across heterogeneous tasks.** An agent that revises both its questions and its experimental strategy from accumulated evidence, with the explicit problem being how to adapt across tasks without overfitting to internal validation. The closest published analogue to an agent driving an ADMET model collection.
- [Apheris, 2026-09-14](https://www.apheris.com/resources/federated-training-dramatically-improves-the-accuracy-of-protein-ligand-co-folding-on-private-pharma-structures) — **Federated training improves protein–ligand co-folding accuracy on private pharma structures.** Industry report that federated training across proprietary structure sets beats public-data-only models. Relevant to any argument that useful models can be built without the data leaving the institution that holds it.

## Global health and open science

- [Tetteh et al., *J Trop Med*, 2026-09-11](https://doi.org/10.1155/jotm/7224187) 🌍 — **Antiplasmodial activity of methylangolensate from *Entandrophragma angolense* stem bark.** Ghana-led work from KNUST and the Noguchi Memorial Institute evaluating multistage antiplasmodial activity of a natural product from local flora, combining in vitro assays with computational characterisation — African-led rather than about Africa.
- [Mangal et al., *J Mol Graph Model*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmgm.2026.109566) — **SilicoXplore: an integrated cloud platform coupling machine learning with physics-based modelling for end-to-end drug discovery.** Hosted platform applied to computational discovery of antimicrobial leads, aimed at groups without local compute. Author affiliations are not resolvable from the record, so no equity marker is applied despite the platform's stated low-resource framing.

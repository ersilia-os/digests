## 🤖 Models that could join the Hub

### 🎯 Activity prediction

- [Shenoy et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-08-03](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.01.742196v1) 🤖💻 — **Nesso-1: Accelerating Open-Source Binding Affinity Predictions.** Open-source activity-prediction model taking SMILES plus target sequence → binding affinity; released with inference code on GitHub under Apache-2.0. Plausible Hub addition because it matches Boltz-2 accuracy at ~1 s per prediction on one GPU, which is the first cofolding-grade affinity model cheap enough for a Hub container. · [code](https://github.com/recursionpharma/nesso) · 🎯
- [Kashyap et al., *Pharmaceuticals*, 2026-07-27](https://doi.org/10.3390/ph19081173) 🤖 — **Continuous Inhibition-Zone Modeling and Binary Classification for *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* Hit Prioritization.** Open-access QSAR model taking SMILES → inhibition-zone diameter (regression) and active/inactive label, published under CC-BY. Plausible Hub addition because it predicts a raw disc-diffusion readout rather than a thresholded MIC, which suits triage of Gram-negative screening decks (weights: pending). · 🎯

### 🧪 Property prediction

- [Sultan et al., *J Cheminform*, 2026-07-29](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13321-026-01252-z) 🤖💻 — **Transformers for molecular property prediction: domain adaptation efficiently improves performance.** Open-source property-prediction model taking SMILES → five ADME endpoints (lipophilicity, permeability, solubility, microsomal clearance, plasma-protein binding); pretrained checkpoints on Hugging Face under CC-BY. Plausible Hub addition because it shows pretraining past ~400–800k molecules stops paying off, so a small domain-adapted checkpoint can replace far heavier ADMET featurizers. · [code](https://huggingface.co/collections/UdS-LSV/domain-adaptation-molecular-transformers-6821e7189ada6b7d0a5b62d4) · 🧪
- [Mansour et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-07-29](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.06.30.735582v2) 🤖 — **ADMET Property Prediction with Quantum-Inspired Preprocessing.** Open-source property-prediction model taking molecular descriptors from SMILES → ADMET endpoints via a CatBoost ensemble fed by simulated many-body Hamiltonian expectation values. Plausible Hub addition because the quantum-inspired step is classical simulation with no hardware lock-in, so it containerises like any descriptor model (weights: pending). · 🧪

### 🧩 Featurization

- [Liu et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-08-05](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.02.26.708362v2) 🤖 — **MolX: A Geometric Foundation Model for Protein-Ligand Modelling.** Graph-transformer featurization model taking SMILES plus pocket geometry → E(3)-equivariant joint embedding, pretrained on over 3 million pockets and 5 million molecules. Plausible Hub addition because compound–protein input passes the small-molecule gate and interface-aware embeddings are absent from current Hub featurizers (weights: pending, infra: heavy). · 🧩
- [Liu et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-08-02](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.29.741404v1) 🤖 — **PG-MLD: Physics-Guided Molecular Representation Learning via Dynamic 3D Trajectory Distillation.** Featurization model taking SMILES → learned embedding, distilling a 3D equivariant teacher into a 1D student so inference needs no conformer generation. Plausible Hub addition because it delivers geometry-aware embeddings at SMILES-only inference cost, the exact trade-off that keeps a featurizer deployable (weights: pending). · 🧩

### 🎨 Generative

- [Hu et al., *J Cheminform*, 2026-08-01](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13321-026-01265-8) 🤖 — **Expanding accessible chemical space for fragment-based enumeration through optimization of the CReM framework.** Open-source generative framework taking SMILES → chemically reasonable mutated analogues, published CC-BY and extending CReM (BSD-3-Clause) with a deduplicated fragment database that cuts storage and query time by over 50 %. Plausible Hub addition because CReM is already a reference generator and oCReM makes billion-scale fragment libraries tractable on modest hardware. · 🎨
- [Tang et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-08-01](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.11.664424v2) 🤖 — **MGMG: Cell Morphology-Guided Molecule Generation for Drug Discovery.** Generative model taking cell-morphology profiles plus text descriptions → novel SMILES, requiring no known target or reference compound. Plausible Hub addition because phenotype-conditioned generation fits screening programmes against pathogens whose druggable targets are unmapped (weights: pending). · 🎨
- [Yang et al., *Research Square*, 2026-07-10](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10212415/v1) 🤖 — **Generative exploration of fragment-based chemical space via large language models (AutoLeadDesign).** Generative framework taking target context → fragment-assembled lead SMILES through an LLM–fragment feedback loop, without target-specific ligand templates. Plausible Hub addition because template-free lead generation is precisely what neglected-disease targets with no known bioactive ligands require (weights: pending). · 🎨
- [Baudino & Stone, *AI Chemistry*, 2026-07-31](https://doi.org/10.3390/aichem1030011) 🤖 — **Machine Learning Integrated Designing and Screening of 8-Hydroxyquinoline Based Metallo-β-Lactamase Inhibitors.** Generative pipeline taking SMILES → NDM-1-biased 8-hydroxyquinoline analogues from a SMILES VAE, with XGBoost docking-score and classification models downstream; published CC-BY. Plausible Hub addition because NDM-1 carbapenemase inhibition is an open Gram-negative AMR gap and the whole workflow is SMILES-in, score-out (weights: pending). · 🎨

## AI/ML methods for drug discovery

- [Ngo et al., *Nature Chemistry*, 2026-08-01](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41557-026-02127-4) ⭐ — **Small-molecule binding-site discovery using silyl ether-enabled chemoproteomics.** Chemoproteomic platform that maps where small molecules actually engage the proteome, giving ligandability evidence for targets that structure prediction cannot reach; directly relevant to prioritising which pathogen proteins are worth modelling at all.
- [Taveneau et al., *Nature Chemical Biology*, 2026-08-01](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41589-025-02136-3) ⭐ — **De novo design of potent CRISPR–Cas13 inhibitors.** AI-designed anti-CRISPR proteins (AIcrs) achieve potent, specific Cas13a inhibition in bacterial and human cells, a clean demonstration that de novo design now beats natural-product mining for some inhibitor classes. (Input: protein sequence — outside the Hub's small-molecule interface.)
- [Ji et al., *Nature Chemical Biology*, 2026-08-01](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41589-026-02237-7) ⭐ — **Generation of membrane-permeable cyclic peptides inhibiting protein–protein interaction.** Cyclic peptides that combine PPI inhibition with genuine membrane permeability, addressing the delivery problem that usually disqualifies peptide leads. (Input: peptide — no SMILES interface.)
- [Schuh et al., *Chemical Science*, 2026-07-22](https://doi.org/10.1039/d6sc01799a) — **Auditing widely used biomolecular benchmarks reveals systematic data inconsistencies.** A systematic audit finds duplicated, mislabelled and leaked records across benchmarks the whole field reports on, which means published gains on these sets partly measure data artefacts; worth reading before trusting any leaderboard used to justify a Hub model.
- [Hu et al., *Advanced Science*, 2026-07-29](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202514899) — **Deep Contrastive Learning for High-Throughput Prediction of Drug Resistance Mutations from Sequences.** DeepMutDTA plus a SimSiam fine-tuning step aligns embedding distance with affinity shift between wild-type and mutant targets, so resistance variants stop being masked by sequence similarity — a template for resistance-aware activity models. (Input: protein sequence.)
- [Karaduman & Yildirim, *Food Chem Toxicol*, 2026-07-29](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fct.2026.116302) — **RenalTox-XAI: applicability-domain-constrained QSAR for drug-induced renal injury.** An explainable nephrotoxicity classifier built from 316 oral drugs and 11 retained descriptors, with an explicit applicability domain; the domain constraint is the transferable idea, though 316 compounds is thin for a general ADMET model.
- [Pinart et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-08-02](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.29.741473v1) 💻 — **MassGAT: graph-based collective learning for untargeted detection and annotation of LC-MS data.** Treats co-eluting ion species of one compound as a graph and infers peak validity and annotation jointly with a graph attention network, beating pipelines that separate the two tasks. (License: none declared in the repository — redistribution status unclear.) · [code](https://github.com/odisce/MassGAT)
- [Fondrie et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-07-30](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.28.741295v1) — **Structure-free, site-resolved contrastive learning extends small-molecule discovery beyond structure-based modeling (Ptarmigan-1).** Co-embeds protein residues and candidate small molecules from sequence and 2D chemistry alone, so cryptic, non-orthosteric and disordered sites become screenable without a pose; a Hub-shaped similarity model if the weights are ever released.
- [Graber et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-07-30](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.27.740774v1) — **How Bias Shapes the Leaderboard: Scoring Function Performance Under Scrutiny.** Dissects how much of machine-learned scoring-function performance comes from pocket and ligand biases rather than binding physics, and pairs naturally with the benchmark audit above when judging any docking-surrogate claim.

## Antibiotic and antimicrobial discovery

- [Deb et al., *Nature Chemical Biology*, 2026-08-03](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41589-026-02269-z) ⭐ — **Computational design of antimicrobial peptide nanopores.** De novo α-helical peptides self-assemble into membrane-spanning nanopores with in vivo efficacy against drug-resistant pathogens, and the work releases 52 modular sequence templates with tunable pore-forming activity — a non-traditional killing mechanism resistance has no prepared answer to.
- [Crunkhorn, *Nat Rev Drug Discov*, 2026-08-01](https://doi.org/10.1038/d41573-026-00105-6) ⭐ — **New antibiotic targets novel ribosomal site.** A new antibiotic class binds a previously unexploited ribosomal site, meaning existing ribosomal resistance determinants do not transfer; a short read that resets what counts as a validated antibacterial target.
- [Karash et al., *Nature Microbiology*, 2026-07-23](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41564-026-02414-3) — **Within-patient gene transfer between transiently and chronically infecting bacteria causes extreme antibiotic resistance during lung infections.** Horizontal transfer inside a single patient produces resistance far beyond either parent strain, which reframes resistance surveillance as a within-host problem rather than a between-patient one.
- [Rehman et al., *Nature Microbiology*, 2026-07-21](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41564-026-02425-0) — ***Klebsiella pneumoniae* inhibits vasodilation through capsule and T6SS-dependent pathways.** Capsule and type VI secretion drive host vascular dysfunction, adding virulence mechanisms to the target list for a pathogen that sits at the centre of Gram-negative AMR priorities.
- [Yelin et al., *Nature Microbiology*, 2026-07-06](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41564-026-02415-2) — **Non-canonical gene amplifications facilitate adaptive evolution in bacteria.** Gene amplification outside canonical duplication routes accelerates bacterial adaptation, a resistance-evolution pathway that genome-based resistance prediction currently ignores.
- [Bond et al., *Nature Communications*, 2025-11-03](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-64662-x) — **Reference-based chemical-genetic interaction profiling to elucidate small molecule mechanism of action in *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*.** Assigns mechanism of action to antitubercular compounds by profiling chemical-genetic interactions against a reference set — the kind of labelled MoA data that antitubercular activity models are usually starved of.
- [Lim, *ChemMedChem*, 2026-07-29](https://doi.org/10.1002/cmdc.70401) — **Aminopeptidase inhibition in drug-resistant *Plasmodium falciparum*: rationale for targeting PfA-M1 and PfA-M17.** Consolidates the structural, functional and pharmacological case for the two *P. falciparum* aminopeptidases as a dual-target pair that stays viable under artemisinin resistance.
- [Leite et al., *ChemMedChem*, 2026-08-01](https://doi.org/10.1002/cmdc.70410) — **Underexplored ligand-binding features of FabI from *Staphylococcus aureus* and *Escherichia coli*.** Comparative pharmacophore modelling and surface mapping expose species differences in the FabI pocket, which is the level of detail needed before a single enoyl-reductase model is trusted across Gram-positive and Gram-negative targets.
- [Biswas et al., *ACS Appl Bio Mater*, 2026-08-01](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsabm.6c00501) 🌍 — **Dinuclear zinc(II) complexes as metallo-β-lactamase inhibitors with antibiofilm activity against MDR Gram-negative bacteria.** Metal-complex inhibitors hit both the carbapenemase and the biofilm, an LMIC-led contribution to the NDM-type resistance problem that dominates Gram-negative AMR in South Asia.
- [Dong & Song, *bioRxiv*, 2026-08-04](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.29.741660v1) — **Evolutionary design of membrane-lytic antimicrobial peptides with mixture of experts (AMPainterV2).** Generative adversarial imitation learning with a mixture-of-experts policy designs membrane-lytic AMPs; all ten top-ranked peptides were active in vitro, the best at 1.5 µM MIC and outperforming polymyxin B. (Input: peptide sequence.)

## AI agents and foundation models for science

- [Connors et al., *Nature Chemical Biology*, 2026-08-01](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41589-026-02272-4) ⭐ — **Designing fiber–gut microbiome interactions with active learning.** A closed-loop active-learning campaign designs interventions in a system too combinatorially large to screen exhaustively — the clearest recent template for applying design-of-experiments loops to microbial phenotypes.
- [Sin et al., *Chem*, 2026-07-01](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chempr.2026.103147) — **Reaction: Navigating the path to reasoning laboratories.** Argues that self-driving labs need reasoning over experimental context, not just closed-loop optimisation, and marks where autonomous-chemistry claims currently overreach.
- [He et al., *ACS Omega*, 2026-07-27](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.6c03486) — **Multiagent data mining for enhanced machine learning prediction of delayed fluorescence materials.** LLM agents extract structured property data from the literature and feed it straight into predictive models; the materials endpoint is off-mission but the agentic curation pattern transfers directly to bioactivity data extraction.

## Global health and open science

- [Agbankpe et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-07-30](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.25.740742v1) 🌍 — **Isolation and characterization of novel *Klebsiella* phages from Benin and their antibiofilm activities on multidrug-resistant and hypervirulent *Klebsiella pneumoniae*.** Phages isolated in Benin and characterised by a Guinea-led team against MDR and hypervirulent clinical strains — locally sourced biologicals for locally circulating resistance, and a reminder that the strain diversity that matters is not in northern collections.
- [Gangwal et al., *Chem Biol Drug Des*, 2026-08-01](https://doi.org/10.1111/cbdd.70372) 🌍 — **Integrating artificial intelligence into drug discovery from medicinal plants: current applications and infrastructural challenges.** An India-led review that treats compute, curated data and skills as the binding constraint on AI-assisted natural-product discovery rather than algorithms — the same infrastructure gap open model registries exist to close.
- [Kumar et al., *BMC Pediatrics*, 2026-07-27](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12887-026-07389-y) — **Geographic and genomic determinants of antimicrobial resistance in Indian children: a systematic review and meta-analysis.** Pools resistance data across Indian paediatric cohorts and links geography to resistance genotype, quantifying the burden in the population where Gram-negative resistance is most lethal. Author affiliations could not be resolved, so no LMIC-led marker is claimed here despite the subject matter.

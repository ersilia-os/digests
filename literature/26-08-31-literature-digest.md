## 🤖 Models that could join the Hub

### 🎯 Activity prediction

- [Li et al., *Interdiscip Sci Comput Life Sci*, 2026-08-26](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12539-026-00867-y) 🤖💻 — **3D Geometric Equivariant Graph Neural Networks with Contrastive Learning for Protein–Ligand Binding Affinity Prediction.** Open-source activity-prediction model taking ligand structure plus protein → binding-affinity regression; released with inference code on GitHub, but the repository carries no licence file, so redistribution needs clarifying before packaging. Plausible Hub addition because affinity regression is the Hub's largest task family (41 % of Ready models) and equivariant 3D encoders are absent from it. · [code](https://github.com/ligaili01/3D-GEGCL) · 🎯
- [Bianchin de Oliveira & Saeed, *bioRxiv*, 2026-08-25](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.24.746801v1) 🤖💻 — **BindScreen: Protein-Centric Contrastive Learning for Sequence-Based Virtual Screening.** Open-source activity-prediction model taking compound SMILES plus target sequence → ranked binding score; released with code on GitHub and checkpoints in a HuggingFace collection, licence not yet declared on either. Plausible Hub addition because it drops the structural prerequisite of docking, making it usable for pathogen targets with no solved structure — exactly the constraint in most NTD campaigns. · [code](https://github.com/pcdslab/BindScreen) · 🎯
- [Liao et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-08-30](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.26.747036v1) 🤖 — **Chemi-Proteome Language Attention Network (C-PLANK) for fragment-based ligand interactome and binding-site discovery.** Open-source activity-prediction model taking fragment/ligand structures → proteome-wide interaction and binding-site predictions, trained against cellular rather than purely in vitro affinity data; weights not yet released and the preprint sits under CC-BY-NC-ND. Plausible Hub addition because proteome-wide fragment profiling would give the Hub a polypharmacology readout it currently lacks. · 🎯
- [Lai et al., *Bioinformatics*, 2026-08](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btag368) 🤖 — **Multimodal contrastive learning for integrating molecular representations and cellular phenotypes in drug–target interaction prediction.** Open-source activity-prediction model taking compound structure plus cell-phenotype context → drug–target interaction score, published CC-BY; weights availability unconfirmed. Plausible Hub addition because fusing structure with Cell-Painting-style phenotypes is the same bridge the Chemical Checker builds, and a packaged version would let Hub users query it directly. · 🎯

### 🧪 Property prediction

- [Luo et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-08-25](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.24.746660v1) 🤖💻 — **Trimole-Hybrid: a multimodal representation learning platform for accurate molecular ADMET prediction.** Open-source property-prediction model taking SMILES → 22 ADMET endpoints, selecting per-task among SMILES-, graph-, 3D- and descriptor-based predictors; source code on GitHub, though the preprint is CC-BY-NC-ND and the repo declares no licence. Plausible Hub addition because it beats the public TDC top-1 method on 10 of 22 tasks, which would put it ahead of several ADMET models already in the Hub. · [code](https://github.com/dchen0212/trimole_hybrid) · 🧪
- [Shouaib et al., *arXiv*, 2026-08-24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23821) 🤖 — **Finch: Toxicity Dose Response Curve Prediction of Chemical Compounds and Mixtures.** Open-source property-prediction model taking compound structures → full dose–response curves, and uniquely handling mixtures rather than single compounds; no repository or weights named yet (weights: pending). Plausible Hub addition because every toxicity model in the Hub is single-compound and single-threshold, so curve-level and mixture-level prediction would be a genuinely new capability. · 🧪

### 🧩 Featurization

- [Rottach et al., *arXiv*, 2026-08-23](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22642) 🤖 — **Mol-JEPA: A multimodal Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture for Molecules.** Open-source featurization model taking SMILES/graph → latent embedding, pretrained by modality masking over 4.69 M molecules spanning binding affinities, ADMET profiles, cell-painting phenotypes and quantum-chemistry data, under CC-BY-4.0 (weights: pending). Plausible Hub addition because it is the closest public analogue to the Chemical Checker's bioactivity-signature idea and would slot straight into the Hub's featurization family. · 🧩
- [Zhao et al., *arXiv*, 2026-08-24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23646) 🤖 — **MolEmb: Multimodal Large Language Models Can Be Strong Molecular Embedding Models.** Open-source featurization model taking SMILES plus a natural-language instruction → conditioned embedding, so one encoder yields task-specific vectors instead of a single fixed representation; no repository named yet (weights: pending). Plausible Hub addition because instruction-conditioned embeddings would let a single Hub featurizer serve property, similarity and retrieval queries. · 🧩
- [Han et al., *arXiv*, 2026-08-26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25893) 🤖 — **A General-Purpose Molecular Foundation Model Transfers Across Diverse Olfactory Tasks.** Open-source featurization model taking SMILES → transferable embedding, built by fine-tuning Uni-Mol2 on the GS-LF odour benchmark and then reused without further training on four downstream tasks (weights: pending). Plausible Hub addition because Uni-Mol2 is a Hub-priority anchor and this is direct evidence that a single fine-tune yields representations that generalise across endpoints. · 🧩

### 🎨 Generative

- [Hussein et al., *J Comput Aided Mol Des*, 2026-08-26](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10822-026-00920-4) 🤖 — **MolGraphGAN: a graph transformer–adversarial framework for target-specific molecule generation.** Open-source generative model taking a target specification → novel molecular graphs, combining a graph transformer with adversarial training; code and weights availability not stated in the article record. Plausible Hub addition because generation accounts for only 5 % of Ready Hub models and target-conditioned graph generators are not represented at all. · 🎨
- [Wang et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-08-27](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.24.745890v1) 🤖 — **RCDO: a closed-loop reinforcement learning framework for rapid compound directed optimization.** Open-source generative model taking a lead series → optimised analogues, with a multi-level reward updated from wet-lab measurements after each design cycle (including inactive and developability-failed compounds); no code statement and the preprint is CC-BY-NC-ND (weights: pending). Plausible Hub addition because prospective campaigns on ROR1, NLRP3 and NSD3 resolved bottlenecks in two to three cycles, and the reward-from-failures design maps well onto small NTD datasets. · 🎨
- [Li et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-08-30](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.26.747172v1) 🤖 — **FlexiTAC: controllable PROTAC linker generation using a Bayesian flow network with posterior guidance.** Open-source generative model taking warhead and ligase-ligand structures → linkers with explicit control over flexibility, attachment site and length, reaching a 79.5–80.0 % PoseBusters pass rate; preprint is CC-BY-NC-ND and no repository is named (weights: pending). Plausible Hub addition because degrader design is central to the `glueAI` work and no Hub model currently generates linkers; its companion corpus is in chapter 2. · 🎨
- [Manu & Alazzwi, *arXiv*, 2026-08-24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24610) 🤖 — **Conditional GraphGANFed: Optimizing Graph-Structured Molecule Generation in Federated Generative Adversarial Networks.** Open-source generative model taking user-defined property targets → molecular graphs, trained federatedly so contributing sites never share raw structures (weights: pending). Plausible Hub addition because federated generation is a concrete answer to partners who cannot export proprietary compound sets, a recurring blocker in collaborative screening. · 🎨

## 🗃️ Datasets that could join the Hub

### Bioactivity datasets

- [Lu et al., *arXiv*, 2026-08-26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26228) 🗃️ — **PathoMIC: A Benchmark for Cross-Species Antimicrobial Peptide Activity Prediction.** 74,751 rows · experimentally reported MIC values across 424 pathogen species · licence not stated · anonymised pre-review repository. Plausible Hub input because MIC corpora at this species breadth are rare, and cross-species generalisation is precisely the failure mode that limits AMR activity models today; note the entries are peptide sequences rather than SMILES, so conversion would be needed. · 🎯

### Generative training corpora

- [Li et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-08-30](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.26.747172v1) 🗃️ — **PROTAC-3D and PROTAC-Bench, released with FlexiTAC.** 63,554 structures · three-dimensional PROTAC conformations plus a standardised linker-generation benchmark · preprint under CC-BY-NC-ND, download host not yet stated. Plausible Hub input because degrader-scale 3D corpora are scarce and a shared benchmark would let the Hub compare linker generators on equal footing. · 🎨

## AI/ML methods for drug discovery

- [Seal et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-08-24](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.19.745868v1) 💻 — **Model Validation Protocols for Machine Learning in Small Molecule Drug Discovery.** Argues that static retrospective benchmarks are instruments rather than goals, and sets out validation protocols for deciding when a property-prediction model will actually hold up in deployment; from the Broad Institute and Polaris, with MIT-licensed code. The most directly useful methods paper this week for how Hub models should be evaluated before incorporation. · [code](https://github.com/srijitseal/polaris)
- [U et al., *Chem Soc Rev*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1039/d5cs01469g) — **AI-powered medicinal chemistry and translational drug development.** Broad review of machine learning, deep learning, NLP and generative modelling across medicinal chemistry, organised by molecular representation — a useful map of which representation families are under-served, and senior-authored by Xingcai Zhang.
- [Ginex & Luque, *J Cheminform*, 2026-08-20](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13321-026-01294-3) — **Assessment of quantum mechanics-based hydropathic descriptors to navigate the isofunctional chemical space in scaffold hopping.** QM-derived hydropathic descriptors are evaluated for retrieving isofunctional replacements, which is the same similarity-search problem the Hub's projection and similarity families address. (Licence: CC-BY-NC-ND, so the descriptors are not redistributable as-is.)
- [Strom et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-08-25](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.24.746585v1) 💻 — **Foldseek-Interface reveals a protein interface universe far from complete.** Converts 3D interface structures into searchable sequences so protein–protein interfaces can be aligned and clustered at interactome scale, with code and a Zenodo deposit. (Input: protein interface structure; licence CC-BY-NC — both block Hub incorporation, but the sequence-encoding trick is worth borrowing for chemical-space search.) · [code](https://github.com/steineggerlab/foldseek-interface-analysis)
- [Kumar et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-08-28](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.27.747591v1) 🌍 — **Coarse composition suffices: tabular in-context learning for multi-activity antimicrobial peptide profiling.** An IIT Roorkee group shows a sequence-only tabular in-context-learning pipeline matches structure-conditioned multimodal models on the ESCAPE multi-label benchmark. (Input: peptide sequence.) A strong argument that TabPFN-style tabular models deserve a place alongside heavyweight encoders — relevant to how the Hub weighs model complexity against data size.
- [Wu et al., *Interdiscip Sci Comput Life Sci*, 2026-08-24](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12539-026-00863-2) — **UniPept: a universal model for predicting peptide properties based on atom-residue dual-scale integration.** Predicts toxicity, antibacterial and anticancer activity from a joint atom- and residue-level view. (Input: peptide sequence.) The dual-scale idea is transferable to small-molecule encoders that currently pick one granularity.
- [Waldock et al., *Front Drug Discov*, 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/drug-discovery/articles/10.3389/fddsv.2026.1894745/full) — **Predictive Accuracy of Peptide–Target Interaction Models in Drug Discovery: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.** Quantifies how inconsistent evaluation metrics have made peptide–target interaction models hard to compare, senior-authored by Cesar de la Fuente-Nunez. A cautionary reference for any Hub benchmarking claim built on published numbers rather than re-run evaluations.
- [Singh et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-08-27](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.26.747357v1) — **CysLENS: interpretable signatures of cysteine ligandability from enantiomeric chemoproteomics and protein language models.** Ranks cysteine ligandability by combining enantiomeric chemoproteomic data with protein language models, yielding interpretable signatures for target triage. Directly relevant to covalent-ligand target selection in the degrader and chemoproteomics threads.

## Antibiotic and antimicrobial discovery

- [Abreu et al., *Lancet Reg Health Am*, 2026-08](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lana.2026.101607) — **Better infectious disease diagnostics is crucial to tackling antimicrobial resistance.** Makes the case that AMR control depends on knowing whether and which antimicrobials are needed, drawing on the Latin American and Caribbean diagnostics network. A reminder that prediction tools only pay off where susceptibility data actually reaches the clinic.
- [Osei et al., *Access Microbiology*, 2026-08-28](https://doi.org/10.1099/acmi.0.001312.v1) 🌍 — **A Multi-Model Comparison of Supervised Machine Learning for Predicting Antimicrobial Resistance in ESKAPE Pathogens.** WACCBIP (University of Ghana) trained Random Forest, XGBoost and an MLP on 12,668 ESKAPE genomes to cut the 24–72 h delay of culture-based susceptibility testing, with Gordon Awandare as senior author. LMIC-led ESKAPE modelling that sits squarely alongside the Gram-negative AMR portfolio.
- [Oduselu et al., *Bioinform Biol Insights*, 2026-08](https://doi.org/10.1177/11779322261483607) 🌍 — **De Novo Molecular Design and Bioactivity Prediction of Novel Hexahydroquinolines as *Plasmodium falciparum* Inhibitors.** A University of Ghana team, senior-authored by Gordon Awandare, couples de novo design with bioactivity prediction on a new antimalarial scaffold series. (Licence: CC-BY-NC, so the generated series and models are not redistributable as-is.) LMIC-led antimalarial design of exactly the kind the Hub should be able to support end to end.
- [Agyapong et al., *BMC Microbiol*, 2026-08-24](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12866-026-05564-w) 🌍 — **Phylogenomics and antimicrobial resistance genes of invasive non-typhoidal *Salmonella* in the Ashanti region of Ghana.** Characterises serovar distribution, MLST profiles and AMR determinants in Ghanaian clinical isolates, senior-authored by Yaw Adu-Sarkodie at KNUST, under CC-BY. Locally generated resistance-gene data of the sort that should anchor, rather than follow, LMIC-facing AMR models.
- [Ojeda et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-08-26](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.25.747168v1) — **Assessing the translation of AI-prioritized genome-derived peptide fragments into validated antimicrobial candidates.** Takes AI-prioritised, genome-mined peptide fragments through to experimental validation against *Staphylococcus aureus*, testing whether predictions survive contact with an assay. The prediction-to-validation attrition rate reported here is the number worth quoting whenever a screening cascade is proposed.
- [Mahdy et al., *J Antibiot*, 2026-08-24](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41429-026-00954-8) — **Lariocidin and ribosome-targeting lasso peptides as emerging antimicrobial agents against multidrug-resistant bacteria.** Reviews lasso peptides that hit structurally and mechanistically novel ribosomal targets, an area where most clinical antibiotics cluster on a handful of mechanisms. Useful framing for which novel-mechanism chemotypes are worth prioritising in antibacterial activity models.
- [Zhang et al., *arXiv*, 2026-08-26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25846) — **Why ML-based cough models do not generalize: a systematic cross-dataset evaluation for tuberculosis screening.** Cough-based TB classifiers reach ROC-AUC up to 0.755 within a dataset but frequently drop below 0.6 externally, indicating the models capture collection artefacts rather than disease acoustics. A clean negative result that argues for cross-dataset validation as a default in any TB screening tool.
- [Spinoza et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-08-30](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.28.747751v1) — **A Low Containment CCHFV Entry Screening Platform Identifies Compounds with Antiviral Activity against Authentic CCHFV.** A glycoprotein-based BSL-2 surrogate assay recovers compounds that also work against authentic Crimean-Congo haemorrhagic fever virus, removing the BSL-4 bottleneck from early screening. Lower-containment surrogates are what make neglected-pathogen screening cascades tractable for partner labs.

## AI agents and foundation models for science

- [Radford et al., *Nature*, 2026-08-26](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10949-y) ⭐ — **Automated prototyping of genetic codes.** A robotic, cell-free platform (AGENTEX) prototypes redesigned genetic codes in multiplex, translating proteins with reassigned codons and non-standard amino acids without editing living genomes; senior-authored by George Church. One of the clearest current demonstrations that lab automation, not model capacity, is the rate limiter in biological design.
- [Li et al., *arXiv*, 2026-08-24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23104) — **Molecular LLM Agents: From Architectural Design to Scientific Autonomy.** Develops a conceptual framework for agents that must perceive and act on chemical objects across SMILES strings, graphs, 3D conformations, spectra, simulations and wet-lab measurements — not just text and code. The most directly applicable agent taxonomy yet for anyone wiring an LLM around a chemistry toolchain.
- [Yu et al., *arXiv*, 2026-08-27](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26753) — **Beyond Execution: Auditing Experimental Fidelity in LLM-Driven Scientific Research.** Shifts evaluation of LLM research agents from "did the code run" to "did the experiment faithfully implement the stated design", auditing fidelity rather than execution. The right lens for judging AI-Scientist-style claims, and a useful check on agent-generated computational results.
- [Zhao et al., *Research Square*, 2026-08-27](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10478855/v2) — **DynaMate: An Autonomous Agent for Protein-Ligand Molecular Dynamics Simulations.** Wraps protein–ligand MD setup, execution and analysis behind an autonomous agent, removing much of the manual expertise that gates MD use. Worth watching as a pattern for exposing heavyweight simulation to non-specialists, which is the same accessibility problem the Hub solves for inference.

## Global health and open science

- [Stürenburg et al., *Nat Commun*, 2026-08-18](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76542-z) ⭐ — **Tracking funding disparities in global health aid with machine learning.** Classifies 3.7 million aid projects worth ~US$332 bn (2000–2022) with LLM-assisted machine learning and compares per-capita health aid against disease burden, finding severe misalignment across Central Africa, South Asia and West Africa. Quantitative evidence on where global health money does and does not follow burden — directly citable in funding and prioritisation arguments.
- [Ntie-Kang et al., *J Cheminform*, 2026-08-24](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13321-026-01280-9) 🌍 — **Computational applications in secondary metabolite discovery (CAiSMD) 2026: focus on secondary metabolites towards drug discovery.** Meeting report from the CAiSMD community, first-authored by Fidele Ntie-Kang at the University of Buea, Cameroon. The clearest annual snapshot of African computational natural-product capacity and a standing point of contact for the GC-ADDA network.
- [*Nature* editorial, 2026-08-26](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02568-4) ⭐ — **South Africa's 'registration law' for scientists could be a template for the world — if the nation gets it right.** A draft law would require some South African scientists to register with a professional body, as engineers and clinicians already do; the editorial backs the principle while criticising the lack of clarity and of community consultation. Consequential for South African partner institutions and for how research accountability gets regulated elsewhere.

## 🤖 Models that could join the Hub

### 🧪 Property prediction

- [von Bülow et al., *chemRxiv*, 2026-11-10](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv.15011954) 🤖💻 — **pKasso: Accurate and accessible prediction of small-molecule protonation states.** Open-source property model taking SMILES → pKa values and pH-dependent protonation-microstate ensembles; released as an installable package with code on GitHub under MIT. Plausible Hub addition because it packages open pKa predictors behind one lightweight interface, a natural pre-processing step for docking and permeability workflows that commercial tools usually cover. · [code](https://github.com/bindresearch/pkasso) · 🧪
- [Benito et al., *chemRxiv*, 2026-11-09](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv.15011871) 🤖💻 — **Assessing the predictive limits of machine learning on cyclic peptides (CycPepNet).** Open-source property-prediction framework taking cyclic-peptide structures (SMILES, descriptors, graphs) → membrane permeability, binding affinity and half-life, benchmarking ten model families across 9,341 peptides; code on GitHub under MIT (weights: pending). Plausible Hub addition because permeability is the one endpoint with a clear predictive signal, and beyond-Rule-of-5 macrocycles are barely covered in the Hub. · [code](https://github.com/molML/CycPepNet) · 🧪

### 🧩 Featurization

- [Lopes Almeida et al., *arXiv*, 2026-10-21](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29589) 🤖❓💻 — **Fragment-Level Graph Encoding for Interpretable Molecular Property Prediction (FRAME).** Conditional Hub candidate via Fine-tuned predictor. Paper contributes: a fragment-based graph representation for GNNs, with code under MIT. Hub would do: train FRAME on a Hub-priority endpoint (e.g. antibacterial activity) → interpretable property predictor that attributes to fragments. Prerequisite: pretrained task weights, which are not yet confirmed. Priority: Medium because the Hub has many GNN featurizers but few that attribute predictions to fragments. · [code](https://github.com/4D-Lab/frame) · 🧩

## AI/ML methods for drug discovery

- [Yi et al., *Chem Rev*, 2026-11-10](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.chemrev.6c00161) — **Diffusion Models for Small-Molecule Design.** Comprehensive review of diffusion-based molecular generation: representations, controllable property- and structure-conditioned design, and how such models are evaluated. A useful map now that Generation is the fastest-growing Hub task family.
- [Moro, *J Chem Inf Model*, 2026-11-12](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.6c03172) — **Ceci n'est pas le benzène: The Treachery of Chemical Representations in Machine Learning for Drug Discovery.** Perspective on the chemical conventions hidden in SMILES, fingerprints, graphs and conformers, and on what models actually see. Worth reading for anyone choosing featurizers for the Hub.
- [Jones et al., *Mol Pharm*, 2026-11-12](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.molpharmaceut.6c01212) — **Novel Molecular Descriptors for ADME Prediction in Beyond Rule of Five Chemical Space.** New descriptors improve ADME prediction for PROTACs and other bRo5 molecules, an area where open Hub models are weak. (Gated: vendor-developed descriptors, no open code.)
- [Li et al., *Adv Mater*, 2026-11-09](https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.75629) — **Discovery of Small-Molecule Cryoprotectants Using Pretrained Language Model Representations.** Pretrained SMILES language models are used as encoders to rank cryoprotectant candidates, which are then confirmed experimentally on red blood cells. A worked example of prospective validation of SMILES encoders. (No code found.)
- [Choudhary et al., *Toxicol Mech Methods*, 2026-11-10](https://doi.org/10.1080/15376516.2026.2758231) 🌍 — **SilicoTox: interpretable machine learning for molecular hazard prioritization.** Separates how much toxicity-prediction accuracy comes from representation, from algorithm and from training-data provenance, across nine toxicity endpoints. This bias analysis is directly relevant to how Hub tox models are benchmarked. (Licence: none stated on the public repository.)
- [Bajorath, *Artif Intell Life Sci*, 2026-12](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ailsci.2026.100194) — **Evaluating the ultimate impact of artificial intelligence on drug discovery.** Perspective on how to judge whether AI has actually changed drug-discovery outcomes. Useful framing for impact reporting on open models.
- [Bai et al., *arXiv*, 2026-11-10](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13945) — **Chem World: A Large-Scale Benchmark and Physics-Aware Framework for Trustworthy Molecular Property Prediction.** Unifies 17 public datasets (over 800,000 instances, 10 property tracks including drug solubility) into one testbed for property models. A 🗃️ candidate once a download link and licence are published.
- [Franz et al., *arXiv*, 2026-11-10](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13398) — **The Dose Makes the Poison: Exposure-Aware Evaluation of Generative Drug Design.** Argues that generated molecules should be ranked by predicted exposure at a realistic dose, not by potency and toxicity scores alone. Relevant to how Hub generators and ADMET models are combined downstream.
- [Bessa et al., *arXiv*, 2026-11-06](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09501) 💻 — **e-means: outperforming k-means (in one-tenth the epochs).** Mini-batch k-means with an exponential moving average and scaling laws that remove the need for hyperparameter sweeps. Released in FAISS under MIT, and practical for clustering large libraries of molecular embeddings, which matters for the Hub's thin projection and similarity families. · [code](https://github.com/facebookresearch/faiss/tree/main/contrib/e_means)
- [García-Cerqueda et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-11-11](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.10.765562) — **Pocket similarity guided selection of protein targets for systematic protein ligand dataset generation.** Uses binding-pocket similarity rather than sequence or fold to choose representative proteins for Target 2035's open binding-data campaign across 2,000+ human proteins (IRB Barcelona). Its data will be a future source for Hub activity models.

## Antibiotic and antimicrobial discovery

- [Williams et al., *Nat Chem*, 2026-11-12](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41557-026-02280-w) ⭐ — **Discovery and development of antimicrobial polymers.** Review of synthetic copolymers that mimic antimicrobial peptides with better synthetic tractability and stability. Useful context for AMR modalities beyond small molecules.
- [Juma et al., *PLOS Med*, 2026-11-13](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1005296) 🌍 — **Analysis of antimicrobial resistance in the LAKANA trial of azithromycin administration in Mali.** Resistance sub-study of a cluster-randomised mass-azithromycin trial in infants, led from the Center for Vaccine Development–Mali. The kind of AMR surveillance evidence that should shape mass drug administration policy in the Sahel.
- [Cai et al., *Cell Rep*, 2026-11](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.118295) — **ZnuB as a targetable vulnerability to combat colistin resistance.** Identifies the zinc-uptake transporter ZnuB as a weak point in colistin-resistant bacteria, a new angle on last-line-antibiotic resistance.
- [Zineddu et al., *J Med Chem*, 2026-11-12](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jmedchem.6c02512) — **Combinatorial Synthesis and Direct-to-Biology Antibacterial Evaluation of Ruthenium(II) and Iridium(III) N^O-Schiff-Base Complexes.** Two combinatorial libraries (176 Ru(II) and Ir(III) complexes) screened directly against bacteria. Metal complexes fall outside most Hub models' applicability domain, which is worth flagging.
- [Kaushik et al., *ACS Infect Dis*, 2026-11-12](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsinfecdis.6c00856) — **Antischistosomal Activity of Quinazolinone Indoles Derived from Asperlicin C.** Two natural-product-derived analogues reach low-micromolar *S. mansoni* IC50 with reasonable microsomal stability and selectivity. Schistosomiasis has almost no Hub coverage.
- [Andrés-Rodríguez et al., *ACS Infect Dis*, 2026-11-06](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsinfecdis.6c00648) — **Integrated Target-Based Screening, Characterization and Computational Profiling of a Natural Alkaloid Library Identifies Tryptanthrin as an Antileishmanial Hit.** A 432-alkaloid screen against *L. donovani* trypanothione reductase yields six dual enzyme and intramacrophage actives, adding labelled data to the kinetoplastid target space.
- [Szczurek et al., *Genome Biol*, 2026-11-14](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-026-04316-3) — **AMP Challenge: towards a standardized, community-wide benchmark for AI-driven antimicrobial peptide design.** A community benchmark for generative antimicrobial peptide design. (Input: peptide sequence, outside the Hub's small-molecule surface.)
- [Duffey et al., *Commun Med*, 2026-11-10](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43856-026-01977-w) — **Comparing malaria transmission blocking efficacy of Primaquine and Tafenoquine with in vivo pre-clinical models.** A head-to-head of the two 8-aminoquinolines on transmission blocking and pharmacokinetics, relevant to transmission-stage activity endpoints.
- [Wang et al., *Microbiol Spectr*, 2026-11-11](https://doi.org/10.1128/spectrum.00786-26) — **Identification of a small molecule that inhibits Pks13 with anti-tuberculosis activity.** B-AP15 inhibits the Pks13 thioesterase domain (IC50 22 µM) in the mycolic-acid pathway. It adds a validated chemotype for a TB target with few public actives.
- [Kimishima et al., *J Antibiot*, 2026-11-09](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41429-026-00972-6) — **A triazole-quinoline derivative of 5-O-mycaminosyltylonolide exhibits potent activity against multidrug-resistant Enterobacterales in combination with SPR741.** A macrolide derivative paired with an outer-membrane potentiator against MDR Gram-negatives, relevant to combination-activity modelling.
- [Sharma et al., *Infect Drug Resist*, 2026-11](https://doi.org/10.2147/idr.s626496) — **Integrating Artificial Intelligence with High-Throughput Biological Testing for Antimicrobial Drug Discovery.** Critical review of graph-based and generative models in antibacterial discovery that were confirmed prospectively, and where they still fall short.
- [Abdou et al., *RSC Adv*, 2026-11-11](https://doi.org/10.1039/d6ra09362k) 🌍 — **A phosphinic acid bioisostere of hydroxyethylamine as a novel antiplasmodial scaffold.** A new antiplasmodial chemotype designed around a peptidomimetic bioisostere, led from Egypt.
- [Panhotra et al., *ChemistrySelect*, 2026-11-10](https://doi.org/10.1002/slct.75132) 🌍 — **Exploring Mercapto-1,2,4-Triazole–Quinoline Hybrids as Antiplasmodial Agents Against Chloroquine-Resistant and Susceptible Plasmodium falciparum Strains.** Quinoline hybrids profiled against both chloroquine-sensitive and chloroquine-resistant strains, led from India, providing paired-strain SAR data for antimalarial activity models.
- [Oh et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-11-09](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.06.764870) — **Thienothiazolocarboxamides: rapidly cidal agents causing catastrophic thiol collapse in Mycobacterium tuberculosis.** A new rapidly bactericidal anti-TB series with a thiol-collapse mechanism, from the NIAID TB Research Section.
- [Uddin et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-11-14](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.13.766473) — **High-Throughput Screen of Advanced Repurposing Library Identifies Compounds Active Against Acute and Chronic Stages of Toxoplasma gondii.** Dual-reporter screen of the ~14,000-compound ReFRAME library against tachyzoites and bradyzoites. If the full screen is deposited, it becomes a Data-to-model route for a parasite the Hub does not cover.

## AI agents and foundation models for science

- [Huang et al., *Commun Chem*, 2026-11-04](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42004-026-02249-3) — **TARMAC as a taxonomy for robot manipulation in chemistry.** Organises the long tail of hands-on lab tasks (setup, adjustment, cleanup) into a structured taxonomy for robotic chemistry. A building block for self-driving labs beyond protocol-level automation.
- [Xiao et al., *J Adv Res*, 2026-11](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jare.2026.11.006) — **GPCRAgent: an intelligent multi-agent platform integrating knowledge graphs and large language models for GPCR-targeted drug discovery.** Multi-agent LLM system grounded in a GPCR knowledge graph, an example of target-family-specific agentic discovery.
- [Agostini et al., *chemRxiv*, 2026-11-11](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv.15011971) — **Hypothesis-driven multi-objective optimization of the Belousov–Zhabotinsky reaction.** Self-driving-lab optimisation steered by explicit hypotheses rather than black-box search, from the Schwaller group.
- [Petitjean et al., *chemRxiv*, 2026-11-09](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv.15011880) — **Orchestrating literature landscaping in drug discovery by coupling citation expansion with large language models.** LLM-assisted systematic literature mapping for drug-discovery projects. Directly relevant to automated literature curation such as this digest.
- [Huynh et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-11-13](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.04.20.719538) — **Human-supervised Agentic AI for Hypothesis Generation and Experimental Assistance in Drug Repurposing.** RepurAgent: a supervisor-and-planner multi-agent system with human oversight that covers repurposing from hypothesis to assay analysis (Uppsala). Its prediction sub-agent is the kind of slot open Hub models can fill.
- [Okekale et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-11-11](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.30.763189) 🌍 — **The Reasoning Paradox in Scientific Sandboxes: Why Extended Reasoning Fails in Autonomous Bioinformatics and How to Fix It.** Forensic audit of reasoning traces from open-weight models on BixBench, explaining why extended reasoning degrades in multi-turn scientific agents. Led from the University of Ghana.

## Global health and open science

- [Fornace et al., *Nat Med*, 2026-10-30](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04722-x) ⭐ — **Climate change threatens malaria elimination in the Asia Pacific.** Argues that acute climate shocks, such as the post-2022-flood surge in Pakistan, threaten elimination more than gradual warming does, which changes how antimalarial pipelines should prioritise endemic settings.
- [Mutono et al., *Commun Med*, 2026-10-09](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43856-026-01958-z) — **Sub-national estimates of visceral leishmaniasis and optimisation of geographical accessibility to treatment centres in Kenya.** Bayesian small-area models on nine years of ward-level surveillance data locate VL hotspots and the best placement of treatment centres. Burden context for kinetoplastid work.
- [Rogers et al., *npj Digit Med*, 2026-11-10](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-026-03380-0) — **Synthetic data to lower barriers towards equitable artificial intelligence in rapid diagnostic test interpretation.** SynSight trains rapid-diagnostic-test readers on synthetic images instead of costly proprietary image libraries, validated on HIV and COVID-19 tests. Synthetic data as an equity lever.
- [Shrestha et al., *PLOS Glob Public Health*, 2026-11-12](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgph.0006510) — **When ownership is not enough: Evaluating the co-development and uptake of the Options Assessment Toolkit (OAT) for Plasmodium vivax radical cure in malaria-endemic countries.** Shows that co-developing a policy tool with countries does not by itself lead to uptake. A lesson for any open tool aimed at national programmes.
- [Saeed et al., *Braz J Biol*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1590/1519-6984.302817) 🌍 — **Smog-driven antimicrobial resistance in Pakistan (2013–2023): a decade of evidence linking PM2.5 peaks, respiratory infections, and MDR/XDR pathogens.** Links air-pollution peaks to respiratory infections and multidrug-resistant pathogens over ten years, an environmental driver rarely included in AMR models.
- [Diaz-Terenti et al., *bioRxiv*, 2026-11-14](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.13.766388) — **Improved long-read sequencing technology reveals full genome architecture of Malian Plasmodium falciparum field isolates.** Complete genomes of recent Malian field isolates rather than lab strains. The first and last authors are not at Malian institutions, an equity gap for work on Malian parasites.

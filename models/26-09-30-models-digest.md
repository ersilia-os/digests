Boiko et al.**<br>Carnegie Mellon University<br>*Nature Machine Intelligence 2025* | Turns a molecule into a 128-dimensional embedding carrying stereoelectronic information — the orbital interactions a quantum-chemical calculation would reveal — without running one. Meijer and colleagues at Wageningen University & Research built RetroMol to give natural products and their biosynthetic gene clusters a shared encoding; it applies curated retrobiosynthetic rules rather than a trained model, and the authors used it to rediscover the nocardichelin B gene cluster and to pair compounds that are biosynthetically similar but structurally dissimilar. Ersilia serves the encoder only: the authors' TabPFN in-context predictor, which needs a labelled support set for each task, is not included. | [Paper](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2608.18982)<br>[Code](https://github.com/blazejba/Monroe) |
